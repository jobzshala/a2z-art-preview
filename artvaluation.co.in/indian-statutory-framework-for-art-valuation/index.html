The Indian Statutes That Govern Art Valuation — And How Each Shapes the Report

An art valuation report in India is a statutory document before it is an aesthetic assessment. The statute the report is written under determines the basis of value, the methodology, the comparables, the format, the disclosures, and the audience. A2Z Valuers prepares art valuation reports under every Indian statute that requires one. This page sets out each framework; the dedicated service pages provide the practice depth for each.

1. Income Tax Act, 1961 — The Primary Art Valuation Statute

The Income Tax Act, 1961 is the single most frequently invoked statute for art valuation in India. A2Z Valuers prepares art valuation reports under the following operational heads:

Section 55(2)(b) — Fair Market Value as on 1 April 2001: For paintings, sculptures, and art assets acquired before 1 April 2001, the cost of acquisition for capital-gain computation is the fair market value on that date. This “base-date valuation” is the single most commonly requested art valuation report in India — required whenever a legacy art collection is sold. The report establishes the 1 April 2001 value using auction records, gallery price lists, and dealer invoices from the relevant period.

Section 48 — Capital Gain Computation: The difference between the sale consideration and the indexed cost of acquisition (original cost or 1 April 2001 fair market value, multiplied by the CBDT Cost Inflation Index for the year of sale divided by the Index for the year of acquisition or 2001–02). A2Z Valuers' capital-gain art valuation reports include the full computation, not merely the asset value.

Section 50C — Stamp-Duty Value Safe Harbour: Applies where the art transaction also involves immovable property — for example, the sale of a gallery space with art included. The stamp-duty value of the property component is treated as the sale consideration if it exceeds the actual consideration, and fair-market-value evidence can rebut this presumption.

Section 56(2) — Income from Other Sources: Where art is received as a gift or at less than fair market value from a non-relative, the difference between fair market value and consideration paid is taxable as income. A2Z Valuers issues fair-market-value certificates for gifted art assets.

Section 34AB — Qualified Valuer Status: Nitesh Shrivastava is registered under Section 34AB as a qualified valuer for art and cultural assets — the prerequisite for issuing valuation reports that the Income Tax Department is obligated to consider in assessments.

Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) — Search and Seizure: For art assets discovered during IT search-and-seizure proceedings, the registered valuer appointed under these sections provides the forensic valuation that determines the declared or undisclosed value of the seized art. Nitesh Shrivastava is registered and appointed under both sections.

2. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016

Under the IBC, 2016, every CIRP requires two independent valuations — one for fair value and one for liquidation value — for all assets in the corporate debtor's estate, including art assets. Art valuations for IBC proceedings are prepared under Regulation 27 of the CIRP Regulations and must comply with the IBBI Valuation Standards and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017.

Fair value — the estimated realisable value of the art asset in an orderly sale between willing, knowledgeable parties.

Liquidation value — the estimated realisable value if the art asset were to be sold within the IBC liquidation timeline — typically a forced-sale scenario that discounts fair value significantly for less liquid art categories.

Avoidance transaction art valuations — where art assets have been transferred by the corporate debtor before the insolvency commencement date under Section 43, 45, 50, or 66 IBC.

For the broader IBC valuation practice, see insolvencyandbankruptcyvaluers.com.

3. Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972

The Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972 (AATA) creates several art valuation requirements:

Compulsory-acquisition compensation under Sections 19–20 — where the Central Government acquires an antiquity compulsorily, the compensation is determined at fair market value. Owners challenging inadequate Government compensation need an independent art valuation benchmarked against auction records to support their counter-claim.

Registration valuation — for ASI registration applications, a current valuation of the antiquity is typically required.

Export permit valuation — the value declared in an ASI export permit application must be supportable by an independent valuation certificate.

For the dedicated art and antiquities practice, see artantiquesvaluers.com.

4. SARFAESI Act, 2002 & Banking Regulation

Where art is pledged as collateral for a bank loan and the borrower defaults, the bank's NPA-resolution and SARFAESI process requires an independent art valuation to establish the current realisable value of the pledged works. A2Z Valuers provides pre-SARFAESI art valuation reports for nationalised and private-sector banks, establishing the reserve price for auction of pledged art assets.

5. Black Money Act, 2015

The Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015 requires the valuation of undisclosed foreign assets held by Indian residents — including foreign-held art collections acquired from undisclosed income. Nitesh Shrivastava is licensed under the Black Money Act to issue such valuations, and prepares reports for assessees responding to Black Money Act notices where their overseas art holdings are in question.

6. PMLA, 2002 — Art Market Compliance

The Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA) now designates Indian art market participants above a threshold as reporting entities — with KYC, record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting obligations. For PMLA compliance purposes, galleries and auction houses may require independent art valuations to establish the fair market value of high-value transactions as part of their anti-money-laundering framework. A2Z Valuers provides PMLA-supporting art valuations for Indian art market participants.
