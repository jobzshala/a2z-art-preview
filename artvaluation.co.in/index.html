Why Indian art valuation must be statute-first

An Art Valuation Report That Does Not Speak the Language of the Indian Authority That Reads It Is No Art Valuation Report at All.

An Assessing Officer reviewing a capital-gain computation needs a report that references the Cost Inflation Index notified by the CBDT, establishes the fair market value as on 1 April 2001 under Section 55(2)(b), and reconciles with the stamp-duty value under Section 50C if applicable. An art valuation report that does not do all three will not survive scrutiny — and will expose the assessee to reassessment, penalty, and interest. A Resolution Professional filing a Regulation 27 report before the NCLT needs a report that delivers fair value and liquidation value under the IBBI Valuation Standards and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 — not a generic market-opinion letter. A2Z Valuers is built for exactly this statutory precision. Every art valuation report issued under Nitesh Shrivastava's signature identifies on its face the Indian statute it is written under, the section it complies with, and the assumptions specific to that section.
