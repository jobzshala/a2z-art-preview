Art Valuation · India

Art Valuation Services for India — Under Indian Statutes

A2Z Valuers accepts art valuation engagements across seven specialist service lines, each prepared under the specific Indian statute the purpose requires. Every report is signed personally by Nitesh Shrivastava and is formatted to satisfy the specific Indian authority that will read it.

1. Capital Gain Art Valuation

1. Capital Gain Art Valuation (Income Tax Act, 1961)

The most frequently requested art valuation in India. The report establishes the fair market value of the art asset as on 1 April 2001 under Section 55(2)(b) (for assets acquired before that date), computes the indexed cost of acquisition using the CBDT Cost Inflation Index, and delivers the complete capital-gain computation under Section 48. For assets acquired after 1 April 2001, the report establishes fair market value on the date of acquisition.

The report is prepared to the standard required by the Assessing Officer, the CIT (Appeals), and the ITAT. For the dedicated capital-gain practice, see capitalgainvaluation.com and incometaxvaluation.com.

Comparables used: Saffronart auction records (primary Indian reference); AstaGuru, Pundole's, and DAG records; Christie's and Sotheby's Indian art sales (international reference); period dealer invoices and gallery price lists for pre-2001 base-date valuations.

  • Typically engaged by: Individual sellers, HNIs, NRIs, Chartered Accountants, tax counsel, estate executors

2. Art Valuation for Insurance

Replacement-cost valuation certificates for art insurance policies issued by Indian insurers — HDFC ERGO, New India Assurance, Bajaj Allianz, ICICI Lombard, Tata AIG, United India Insurance — and international insurers operating in India. The certificate establishes the sum insured, the basis of settlement (agreed value or market value), and the condition of the insured work at the date of the certificate.

For scheduled policies, each work is individually valued; for blanket policies, the aggregate portfolio value is established. For the full art insurance advisory, see artinsurancepolicy.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Collectors, galleries, corporate art managers, insurers, brokers

3. Estate & Family Division Art Valuation

Equitable-distribution valuations for inheritance, partition, family settlement, and probate of art collections before Indian courts — District Courts, High Courts, family courts, and probate courts. The report is formatted to satisfy the court's evidentiary requirements and the executor's duty of fair dealing with all beneficiaries. Where the estate includes art assets subject to the Antiquities Act, the report additionally identifies Antiquities Act registration status. For estate art asset-level appraisal, see artassetsappraiser.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Estate executors, beneficiaries, family court counsel, probate courts

4. IBC / NCLT Art Valuation

Regulation 27 fair-value and liquidation-value reports for art assets held by corporate debtors in CIRP proceedings — for Resolution Professionals, the Committee of Creditors, and the NCLT. Reports drafted under the IBBI Valuation Standards and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, in the format the NCLT requires. Art assets in corporate-debtor estates include corporate art collections, gallery businesses in CIRP, and personal art holdings pledged to financial creditors. For broader IBC practice, see insolvencyandbankruptcyvaluers.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Resolution Professionals, financial creditors, operational creditors, NCLT-appointed liquidators

5. Bank-Empanelled Art Valuation

Art and cultural asset valuations for nationalised and private-sector banks — for credit decisions where art is offered as collateral, NPA-resolution proceedings, SARFAESI proceedings, and DRT applications. The report establishes current fair market value of the pledged artwork, a liquidation-value estimate for the bank's collateral-coverage analysis, and a re-valuation schedule (recommended annually for actively-traded art categories). A2Z Valuers is empanelled with leading Indian banks for art and luxury asset valuation.

  • Typically engaged by: Bank credit officers, NPA resolution teams, SARFAESI authorised officers, DRT counsel

6. Donation & Museum Gift Art Valuation

Donation valuations for gifts of art to the NGMA (New Delhi and Mumbai), CSMVS (Mumbai), National Museum New Delhi, state museums, folk art foundations, university libraries, and literary archives — structured to satisfy the donee institution's acquisition requirements and the donor's Income Tax Act position (Section 80G deduction where applicable). For the full donation valuation practice, see giftvaluation.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Donors, museum acquisition teams, cultural trusts, charitable institutions

7. Litigation & Forensic Art Valuation

Reports for Indian courts, NCLT, ITAT, DRT, IT and GST search-and-seizure proceedings, ASI compulsory-acquisition compensation proceedings, matrimonial disputes, and arbitration. Nitesh Shrivastava is registered under Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) of the Income Tax Act and has been appointed by the Income Tax Department, GST Department, Narcotics Department, and Customs Department to appraise art during PAN India enforcement proceedings. His forensic art valuation reports meet the evidentiary standard of the Delhi High Court, the ITAT, the NCLT, and the DRT.

  • Typically engaged by: IT/GST/Customs respondents, court-appointed experts, matrimonial litigants, arbitrators, Resolution Professionals
