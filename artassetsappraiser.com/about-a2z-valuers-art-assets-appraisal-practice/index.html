What makes art asset appraisal different from art valuation

The distinction between an art asset appraisal and a conventional art valuation is substantive, not merely semantic:

Purpose orientation — an art valuation tells the owner what a piece is worth for a defined purpose (insurance, capital gain, estate). An art asset appraisal addresses the asset's role in a financial portfolio — its contribution to net worth, its collateral value, its liquidity profile, its income potential, and its realisation timeline.

Portfolio-level analysis — art asset appraisal operates at the portfolio level, not the individual-work level. It delivers an asset register with individual valuations, aggregate portfolio value, liquidity tier classification (highly liquid / moderately liquid / illiquid), and market-timing advice — the outputs a CFO or family office CIO needs.

Accounting framework alignment — for corporate art collections, the appraisal must align with Ind AS 16 (Property, Plant and Equipment) or Ind AS 38 (Intangible Assets), including useful-life assessment and impairment analysis — outputs a conventional art valuation does not provide.

Collateral-specific deliverables — for art-backed lending, the appraisal must include a liquidation value (the value achievable in a forced sale within 90 days), a loan-to-value recommendation, and a monitoring schedule — outputs a standard insurance valuation does not provide.

IBC-specific methodology — for art assets in CIRP proceedings, the appraisal must deliver fair value and liquidation value under Regulation 27 of the IBC CIRP Regulations, in the format the NCLT requires — outputs a standard art valuation does not provide.
