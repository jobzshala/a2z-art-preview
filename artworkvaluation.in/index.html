Why Indian artwork valuation must be statute-first

An Artwork Valuation Report That Does Not Identify Its Statutory Framework Is Not a Valuation Report. It Is an Opinion.

The distinction is not pedantic — it is legally decisive. An Assessing Officer reviewing a capital-gain computation for the sale of a painting needs a report that references Section 55(2)(b) of the Income Tax Act (fair market value as on 1 April 2001), applies the CBDT Cost Inflation Index for the relevant years, and — where a gallery premises sale is involved — reconciles with Section 50C (stamp-duty value). An artwork valuation report that does not do all three will not be accepted in scrutiny. A Regulation 27 CIRP report filed before the NCLT must comply with the IBBI Valuation Standards and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 — not a generic market-opinion letter. A SARFAESI reserve-price report must align with the RBI’s empanelled-valuer guidelines. A2Z Valuers is built for exactly this statutory precision. Every artwork valuation report issued under Nitesh Shrivastava’s signature identifies on its face the Indian statute it is written under, the section it complies with, and the assumptions specific to that section — because that identification is what makes the report actionable by the authority that receives it.
