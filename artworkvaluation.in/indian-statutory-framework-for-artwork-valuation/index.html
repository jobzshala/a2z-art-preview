The Indian Statutes That Govern Artwork Valuation — And How Each Shapes the Report

In India, an artwork valuation report is a statutory document before it is an aesthetic assessment. The statute under which the report is written determines the basis of value, the methodology, the comparables sequence, the format, the disclosures required, and the authority that will accept it. A2Z Valuers prepares artwork valuation reports under every Indian statute that requires one. This page sets out six such frameworks.

1. Income Tax Act, 1961 — The Primary Artwork Valuation Statute in India

The single most frequently invoked statute for artwork valuation in India. A2Z Valuers prepares artwork valuation reports under the following specific sections:

Section 55(2)(b) — Fair Market Value as on 1 April 2001: For paintings, sculptures, prints, folk art, and all artwork categories acquired before 1 April 2001, the cost of acquisition for capital-gain computation is the fair market value on that date. This base-date artwork valuation is the single most commonly requested report in the Indian art market. It requires comparables drawn from auction records, gallery price lists, dealer invoices, and period documentation from the 1990s and early 2000s. A2Z Valuers maintains a historical comparables database specifically for this purpose.

Section 48 — Capital Gain Computation: The concluded capital gain is the difference between the sale consideration and the indexed cost of acquisition — the 1 April 2001 fair market value (or actual acquisition cost for post-2001 purchases) multiplied by the CBDT Cost Inflation Index for the year of sale divided by the Index for the year of acquisition or 2001–02. A2Z Valuers’ capital-gain artwork valuation reports include the full Section 48 computation, not merely the asset value.

Section 56(2) — Income from Other Sources: Where artwork is received as a gift or at less than fair market value from a non-relative, the difference between fair market value and consideration paid is taxable as income. A2Z Valuers issues fair-market-value artwork valuation certificates for gifted artwork for this purpose.

Section 34AB — Qualified Valuer Registration: Nitesh Shrivastava is registered under Section 34AB as a qualified valuer for artworks and cultural assets — the statutory prerequisite for issuing artwork valuation reports that the Income Tax Department is obligated to consider in assessments and not merely note.

Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) — Search and Seizure: For artworks discovered during Income Tax search-and-seizure proceedings, the Section 132 registered valuer appointed by the IT Department provides the forensic artwork valuation that determines the declared or undisclosed value of the seized works. Nitesh Shrivastava is registered and has been appointed under both sections for PAN India enforcement proceedings.

Black Money Act, 2015: For artwork held overseas by Indian residents as undisclosed foreign assets, Nitesh Shrivastava is licensed under the Black Money Act to issue artwork valuations for assessees responding to Black Money Act notices where foreign-held art collections are in question.

2. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016

Under the IBC, 2016, every CIRP requires two independent valuations — fair value and liquidation value — for all assets in the corporate debtor’s estate, including artworks held by the debtor or pledged to creditors. A2Z Valuers’ IBC artwork valuations are prepared under:

Regulation 27 of the CIRP Regulations — governing the two-valuer requirement and report format.

IBBI Valuation Standards — the methodology framework prescribed for registered valuers in IBC proceedings.

Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 — the qualification and reporting standards for registered valuers.

For artwork in a CIRP estate, the fair value is the estimated realisable value in an orderly sale between willing, knowledgeable parties; the liquidation value is the estimated realisable value if the artwork is sold within the IBC liquidation timeline — a forced-sale scenario that discounts fair value significantly for less-liquid artwork categories. A2Z Valuers explicitly models this discount for every artwork category in its IBC reports. For the broader IBC practice, see insolvencyandbankruptcyvaluers.com.

3. Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972

The Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972 (AATA) creates artwork valuation requirements at three points:

Compulsory-acquisition compensation (Sections 19–20) — where the Central Government acquires an artwork compulsorily, compensation is determined at fair market value. Owners challenging inadequate compensation need an independent artwork valuation benchmarked against auction records to support their counter-claim before the Collector and the courts.

Registration valuation — a current valuation of the antiquity is required for ASI registration applications, establishing the declared value at the date of registration.

Export permit valuation — the value declared in an ASI export permit application must be supportable by an independent artwork valuation certificate. Where the declared value is challenged by the ASI, an independent valuation provides the owner’s evidentiary response.

For the dedicated art and antiquities valuation practice, see artantiquesvaluers.com.

4. SARFAESI Act, 2002 & Banking Regulation

Where artwork is pledged as collateral for a bank loan and the borrower defaults, the bank’s NPA-resolution and SARFAESI process requires an independent artwork valuation to establish the current realisable value of the pledged works and set the reserve price for auction. A2Z Valuers provides SARFAESI-supporting artwork valuations for nationalised and private-sector banks, formatted to align with the RBI’s empanelled-valuer guidelines and the bank’s internal NPA-resolution framework.

5. Companies Act, 2013

Under Section 247 of the Companies Act, 2013, artwork held on a company’s balance sheet requires a registered-valuer report for schemes of arrangement, restructuring, and statutory disclosures filed with the Registrar of Companies and the NCLT. A2Z Valuers issues Section 247 artwork valuation reports for corporate-owned art collections under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017.

6. PMLA, 2002 — Art Market Compliance

The Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA) now designates Indian art market participants above a transaction threshold as reporting entities — with KYC, record-keeping, and suspicious transaction reporting obligations. For PMLA compliance purposes, galleries, auction houses, and dealers may require independent artwork valuations to establish fair market value as part of their anti-money-laundering customer due diligence framework. A2Z Valuers provides PMLA-supporting artwork valuations for Indian art market participants.
