Artwork Categories Valued Across India — Under Indian Statutory Frameworks

Indian Modern Masters — Highest Indian Market Liquidity

Works by the Progressive Artists’ Group and their generation — M.F. Husain, F.N. Souza, S.H. Raza, V.S. Gaitonde, Tyeb Mehta, Krishen Khanna, Ram Kumar, Akbar Padamsee, Bhupen Khakhar — constitute the most liquid and best-documented category in the Indian art market. For capital-gain purposes, this category has the deepest Indian auction comparables record — AstaGuru and Saffronart sales from 2007 to the present, and Pundole’s records stretching further back — making the 1 April 2001 base-date valuation achievable with documented support.

The Christie’s sale of M.F. Husain’s Untitled (Gram Yatra) at ₹119 crore on 19 March 2025 is the apex benchmark. For IBC liquidation-value purposes, this category commands the highest liquidity ratio in Indian art — typically 55–70% of fair value realisable within the CIRP timeline. See modernartvaluers.com and indianmordernartvaluers.com.

Bengal School & Pre-Modern Indian Art

Tagore, Nandalal Bose, Jamini Roy, Abanindranath Tagore, and the Bengal School. A significant collector base in Kolkata and the broader eastern Indian market. For capital-gain purposes, Bengal School works frequently require 1 April 2001 base-date valuations, as many entered current collections in the 1980s and 1990s. Primary Indian comparables: AstaGuru and Saffronart Bengal School categories; Pundole’s and Christie’s historical sale records.

Contemporary Indian Art

Post-2000 Indian contemporary — Subodh Gupta, Atul Dodiya, Jitish Kallat, Bharti Kher, Jagannath Panda. Active secondary market on AstaGuru and Saffronart; primary-market gallery data from DAG, Vadehra, Chemould, Sakshi, and Apparao. For capital-gain purposes, the 1 April 2001 base-date issue does not arise for works by living artists acquired post-2001; the methodology is straightforward. See contemporaryartvaluers.com.

Mughal, Rajput & Miniature Paintings

Court miniatures, Mughal, Deccani, Rajput, and Pahari paintings. These are antiquities under the AATA for objects over 100 years old — adding an Antiquities Act compliance layer to every income-tax, insurance, or estate valuation. Christie’s and Sotheby’s Islamic and Indian Art sales are the primary international comparables; AstaGuru provides Indian auction data. For Indian capital-gain purposes, these works are frequently the subject of 1 April 2001 base-date requests. See artantiquesvaluers.com.

Folk & Tribal Indian Art

Madhubani, Warli, Gond, Phad, Kalamkari, Kalighat, Pichwai, Patachitra, Kerala Murals, and other regional traditions. Growing institutional acquisition category (NGMA, state museums, craft foundations) and active private-collector market. For Indian capital-gain purposes, the absence of a deep auction record makes comparables research intensive; A2Z Valuers draws on NGMA acquisition records, Crafts Council of India documentation, government scheme pricing, and specialist dealer records. AstaGuru’s folk art auctions provide the most directly relevant Indian comparables.

Decorative Arts, Textiles & Photography

Mughal carpets, colonial silver, Anglo-Indian furniture, ceramics, Kashmiri shawls, Paithani and Kanchipuram textiles, and fine-art photography (Raghu Rai, Dayanita Singh). Valued under the same Income Tax Act and insurance frameworks as fine art, with category-specific comparables from Bonhams, AstaGuru, and specialist dealer records. For textiles and carpets, see rugscarpetsvaluation.com and textilesvaluation.com.
