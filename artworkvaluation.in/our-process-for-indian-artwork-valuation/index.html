Art Valuation · India

From First Call to Signed Report — Our Six-Step Indian Artwork Valuation Process

01

Brief

A confidential conversation about the artwork, the Indian statute the report is being drafted under (Income Tax Act, IBC, Antiquities Act, SARFAESI, insurance requirement), the specific authority the report is for (Income Tax Department, NCLT, bank, insurer, court), and the deadline. We confirm acceptance and indicate fee and timeline.

02

Engagement Letter

Written engagement letter setting out scope, statutory framework, basis of value (fair market value, replacement cost, or liquidation value), deliverable, fee, and turnaround. No artwork valuation work begins without a signed engagement letter.

03

Indian Statutory Framework Identification

The defining step of every Indian artwork valuation engagement. The applicable statutory framework is confirmed in writing: which Income Tax Act section governs the valuation, whether the CBDT Cost Inflation Index applies and for which Index years, whether the artwork is an antiquity subject to the AATA, whether IBBI Valuation Standards apply, and which Indian comparables databases are primary for this category. This written confirmation is shared with the client before physical inspection begins — so that every subsequent step in the process is oriented to the Indian authority that will receive the report.

04

Physical Inspection

Physical inspection of the artwork in situ. Photography under standard and raking light. Condition assessment using the category-specific protocol. Provenance documentation reviewed and recorded. For artworks that may be antiquities under the AATA, registration status is verified with the ASI.

05

Indian Comparables Research

Comparables drawn from Indian auction records first — AstaGuru, Saffronart, Pundole’s, and DAG as primary Indian references; Christie’s and Sotheby’s Indian art sales as secondary international references. For folk and decorative arts: NGMA acquisition records, Crafts Council of India documentation, and specialist dealer records. CBDT Cost Inflation Index applied where the engagement is a capital-gain computation, with the specific Index values for the relevant years stated in the report.

06

Signed Artwork Valuation Report

The final artwork valuation report on A2Z Valuers letterhead, signed personally by Nitesh Shrivastava, with the Government Approved Valuer’s certificate of registration under the relevant Indian statute. Formatted specifically for the Indian authority that will read it — whether that is an Assessing Officer, an NCLT bench, a bank credit committee, or a court-appointed expert examiner. Digital and bound hard copy as standard.

Confidentiality

Every engagement is conducted under a strict confidentiality undertaking enforceable under Indian law. Artwork details, valuation figures, and the existence of the engagement are not disclosed to any third party without written client consent.
