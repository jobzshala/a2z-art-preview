Artwork Valuation Services for India — Under the Indian Statutes That Require Them

A2Z Valuers accepts artwork valuation engagements across seven specialist service lines, each prepared under the specific Indian statute the purpose requires and formatted for the specific Indian authority that will receive it. Every report is signed personally by Nitesh Shrivastava, Government Approved Valuer.

1. Capital Gain Artwork Valuation

1. Capital Gain Artwork Valuation (Income Tax Act, 1961)

The most frequently requested artwork valuation service in India. When an artwork is sold, the seller’s capital gain is the difference between the sale consideration and the indexed cost of acquisition. For artworks acquired before 1 April 2001 — which includes the majority of long-held Indian art collections — the cost of acquisition is the fair market value as on 1 April 2001 under Section 55(2)(b). A2Z Valuers’ capital-gain artwork valuation reports deliver: the base-date fair market value with comparables support; the indexed cost of acquisition using the CBDT Cost Inflation Index for the relevant years; and the complete Section 48 capital-gain computation. For the Christie’s benchmark — M.F. Husain’s Untitled (Gram Yatra) sold at ₹119 crore on 19 March 2025 — and for AstaGuru and Saffronart records across the Indian modern market, A2Z Valuers maintains a continuously updated Indian auction comparables database for capital-gain purposes. For dedicated practice, see capitalgainvaluation.com and incometaxvaluation.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Individual sellers, HNIs, NRIs, Chartered Accountants, tax counsel, estate executors

2. Artwork Valuation for Insurance

Replacement-cost artwork valuation certificates for policies issued by Indian art insurers — HDFC ERGO, New India Assurance, Bajaj Allianz, ICICI Lombard, Tata AIG, and United India Insurance — and international insurers operating in India. The certificate establishes the sum insured per artwork, documents condition at the date of the certificate, and is formatted to the insurer’s preferred presentation standard. Annual or biennial re-valuation is recommended for actively-traded artwork categories (Indian modern and contemporary) where market values move materially within 12–24 months. For the full insurance practice, see artinsurancepolicy.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Collectors, galleries, corporate art managers, insurers, brokers

3. Estate & Family Division Artwork Valuation

Equitable-distribution artwork valuations for inheritance, partition, family settlement, and probate of artwork collections before Indian courts. The report is formatted to satisfy the court’s evidentiary requirements and the executor’s duty of fair dealing. Where the estate includes artworks subject to the Antiquities Act (works over 100 years old), the report additionally identifies ASI registration status and flags unregistered antiquities requiring disclosure. For estate-level art asset portfolio assessment, see artassetsappraiser.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Estate executors, beneficiaries, family courts, probate courts, family counsel

4. IBC / NCLT Artwork Valuation

Regulation 27 fair-value and liquidation-value reports for artworks in corporate-debtor estates in CIRP proceedings — for Resolution Professionals, the Committee of Creditors, and the NCLT. A2Z Valuers’ IBC artwork valuation reports are prepared under the IBBI Valuation Standards and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017, in the NCLT-required format. The report explicitly models the liquidation discount applicable to each artwork category under the CIRP timeline constraint — Indian modern masters at 55–70% of fair value; folk art and decorative arts at 30–50%; emerging and digital art at 20–40%. See insolvencyandbankruptcyvaluers.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Resolution Professionals, financial creditors, NCLT-appointed liquidators, corporate debtors

5. Bank-Empanelled Artwork Valuation

Artwork and cultural asset valuations for nationalised and private-sector banks — for credit decisions where artwork is offered as collateral, NPA-resolution proceedings, SARFAESI reserve-price setting, and DRT applications. A2Z Valuers is empanelled with leading Indian banks for art and luxury asset valuation. Reports are formatted to align with the RBI’s empanelled-valuer guidelines and the bank’s internal collateral-management framework, with a current fair market value, an estimated liquidation value, and a recommended re-valuation schedule for the bank’s collateral-monitoring purposes.

  • Typically engaged by: Bank credit officers, NPA-resolution teams, SARFAESI authorised officers, DRT counsel

6. Donation & Museum Gift Artwork Valuation

Donation valuations for gifts of artwork to the NGMA (New Delhi and Mumbai), CSMVS (Mumbai), National Museum New Delhi, state museums, folk art foundations, university libraries, and literary archives — structured to satisfy the donee institution’s formal acquisition requirements and the donor’s Section 80G Income Tax Act position where applicable. The valuation must be independent, documented, and issued by a Government Approved Valuer whose credential is acceptable to both the Indian tax authority and the institution’s board. See giftvaluation.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Donors, museum acquisition teams, cultural trusts, Section 80G-registered institutions

7. Litigation & Forensic Artwork Valuation

Reports for the Delhi High Court, other High Courts, NCLT, ITAT, DRT, IT and GST search-and-seizure proceedings, ASI compulsory-acquisition compensation proceedings, customs assessments, matrimonial disputes, and arbitration. Nitesh Shrivastava is registered under Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) of the Income Tax Act and has been appointed by the Income Tax Department, GST Department, Narcotics Department, and Customs Department to conduct artwork valuations during PAN India enforcement proceedings. Every forensic artwork valuation report is prepared to the evidentiary standard of the specific forum — with methodology, comparables, and conclusions documented to withstand cross-examination by opposing experts or scrutiny by a bench-level court or tribunal.

  • Typically engaged by: IT/GST/Customs respondents, matrimonial litigants, estate executors, Resolution Professionals, courts
