A2Z Valuers — The Government Approved Arts Valuation Practice

The practice

A2Z Valuers is a Delhi-headquartered valuation practice led by Nitesh Shrivastava — Civil Engineer, Government Approved Valuer, and Proprietor. The practice operates across 250+ professional valuation services across India — and within its arts valuation specialism, the defining characteristic is breadth: the ability to value every category of arts asset the Indian and international market transacts, under every valuation purpose that requires a formally issued Government Approved certificate.

The practice was built on the recognition that the Indian arts market is far wider than oil paintings and Mughal miniatures — and that every arts asset, however unusual, deserves the same quality of defensible valuation as the most traded fine art. From a Gond tribal painting acquired at a craft fair to a first-edition Tagore manuscript, from a Bollywood golden-era lobby card to a digital generative artwork, A2Z Valuers issues the valuation report that makes the asset legally manageable.

Nitesh Shrivastava

  • Nitesh Shrivastava
  • Civil Engineer | Government Approved Valuer | Proprietor, A2Z Valuers

Nitesh Shrivastava is a Civil Engineer by training and a Government Approved Valuer by profession with over two decades of arts valuation experience across India and internationally. As an independent arts appraiser for archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures, works of art, and luxury items, he has valued arts assets across the full spectrum — Indian modern masters, Bengal School artists, Mughal and Rajput court painters, folk and tribal art traditions including Madhubani, Warli, Gond, Phad, and Kalamkari; decorative arts in ceramics, silver, textiles and carpets; performing arts memorabilia; literary manuscripts and rare books; vintage musical instruments; fine-art photography; vintage cinema memorabilia; and digital and new media arts.

His valuation reports are accepted by the Income Tax Department, NCLT, the High Court, nationalised and private banks, insurers, and embassies. Comparables are benchmarked against the catalogues of Christie's, Sotheby's, Bonhams, Phillips, Saffronart, AstaGuru, Pundole's and DAG, and against specialist comparables databases for folk art, decorative arts, musical instruments, and memorabilia.

Our standards for arts valuation

Category-specific methodology first — a Madhubani valuation is not conducted like a Husain valuation; a musical instrument valuation is not conducted like a decorative-arts valuation. Each arts category has its own primary value drivers, its own comparables sources, and its own condition assessment protocol.

Provenance research calibrated to the category — for folk art, the artist's community, training lineage, and institutional recognition are primary provenance factors; for performing arts memorabilia, documentation of association with the performer or production is the primary factor.

Comparables drawn from the most relevant venue for each category — folk art comparables from NGMA acquisition records and craft fair auction results; fine art comparables from Christie's and Saffronart; memorabilia comparables from international specialist auction houses; musical instrument comparables from Bonhams Musical Instruments and Indian dealers.

Signed and stamped reports on letterhead with the principal valuer's certificate of registration, in the format required by the purpose and the authority.
