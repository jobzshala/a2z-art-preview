Arts Categories A2Z Valuers Values — With Methodology and Comparables for Each

1. Fine Art & Paintings

The foundational arts category — and the most actively traded in the Indian market. A2Z Valuers values fine art across the full spectrum: Indian modern masters (Husain, Souza, Raza, Gaitonde, Tyeb Mehta, Krishen Khanna, Ram Kumar, Akbar Padamsee and the Progressive Artists’ Group), Bengal School (Tagore, Nandalal Bose, Jamini Roy), Mughal and Rajput court miniatures, Deccani and Pahari paintings, contemporary Indian art (Subodh Gupta, Atul Dodiya, Jitish Kallat, Bharti Kher), European old masters, sculpture, drawings, prints, and works on paper.

Comparables benchmarked against Christie's, Sotheby's, Bonhams, Phillips, Saffronart, AstaGuru, Pundole's and DAG. For the dedicated fine-art and painting practice, see a2zartappraiser.com and paintingvaluers.com.

2. Folk & Tribal Indian Arts

Indian folk and tribal arts represent one of the world's richest and most culturally significant visual traditions — and one of the most under-valued in formal valuation practice. A2Z Valuers is one of the very few Government Approved Valuation practices in India to issue formally defensible valuation reports for folk and tribal arts. The practice values:

Madhubani / Mithila painting — Bihar's ancient tradition, from ritual wall painting to paper and canvas works by masters including Sita Devi, Ganga Devi, and Bharati Dayal. See madhubanipaintingvaluer.com.

Warli painting — Maharashtra's geometric tribal tradition. See warlipaintingvaluer.com.

Gond art — Madhya Pradesh's intricate dot-and-dash tradition, with masters including Jangarh Singh Shyam and the Tekam, Dhurve families. See gondpaintingvaluer.com.

Phad painting — Rajasthan's narrative scroll tradition. See phadpaintingvaluer.com.

Kalamkari — Andhra Pradesh's pen-and-block-printed textile and paper tradition. See kalamkaripaintingvaluer.com.

Kalighat painting — Kolkata's 19th-century Bengal bazaar tradition. See kalighatpaintingvaluer.com.

Pichwai painting — Nathdwara's devotional temple-hanging tradition. See picchwaipaintingvaluer.com.

Patachitra — Odisha's painted cloth scroll tradition. See patachitrapaintingvaluer.com.

Kerala Murals — the classical temple fresco tradition of Kerala. See keralamuralspaintingvaluer.com.

Additional traditions: Thangka, Tanjore, Mysore, Pichvai, Ganjifa, Sanjhi, and other regional arts.

Comparables sources for folk arts: NGMA acquisition records, Crafts Council of India documentation, government scheme price schedules, specialist dealer records, and auction results from AstaGuru's folk art auctions.

3. Decorative Arts

Decorative arts occupy the productive space between fine art and craft — objects that are simultaneously aesthetically significant and functionally derived. In the Indian market, this category encompasses:

Ceramics and pottery — Mughal blue-and-white, Jaipur blue pottery, studio ceramics by contemporary Indian potters.

Silver and metalwork — colonial-era Indian silver, Bidriware, Dhokra, Pemberton-era silver.

Textiles and carpets — Mughal and Indo-Persian carpets, Kashmiri shawls, Zardozi embroidery, Paithani and Kanchipuram textiles. See rugscarpetsvaluation.com and textilesvaluation.com.

Furniture — Anglo-Indian colonial furniture, inlaid and lacquered furniture, art deco pieces. See decorativeartfurniturevaluers.com.

Jade and hardstone — Mughal jade objects, hardstone carvings, and rock crystal pieces.

Glass and ceramics — antique and studio glass, Wedgwood, Royal Doulton.

Comparables: Bonhams Indian and Islamic decorative arts; Christie's and Sotheby's Islamic art and Indian furniture; AstaGuru's vintage and decorative arts auctions.

4. Performing Arts Memorabilia

Performing arts memorabilia is one of the fastest-growing arts asset categories globally — and in India, it encompasses a uniquely rich tradition across Bollywood, classical music, classical dance, and theatre. The valuation of performing arts memorabilia requires a methodology that accounts for rarity, direct association with the performer or production, provenance documentation, condition, and the current collector-market appetite for the specific artist or production.

Film props and costumes — objects used in specific films by specific actors; valuations supported by production documentation and film archives.

Vintage Bollywood posters and lobby cards — original-print lithograph posters from the 1940s–1980s; an actively traded category at AstaGuru and specialist dealers.

Autographed memorabilia — programmes, photographs, scripts, and objects signed by significant performers; authentication and provenance are primary value factors.

Stage costumes — from significant productions in classical dance, theatre, and opera; institutional and collector market.

Concert memorabilia — posters, programmes, instruments, and personal effects associated with significant Indian classical or film music performances.

Comparables: AstaGuru's memorabilia auctions; international specialist auction houses (Sotheby's Entertainment Memorabilia, Christie's); specialist dealer records.

5. Literary Arts

Literary arts represent the most under-valued arts category in the Indian formal valuation market — and one where the gap between intrinsic significance and documented market value is widest. A2Z Valuers values:

Manuscripts — Persian, Sanskrit, Arabic, Urdu, Hindi, and regional-language manuscripts, including illustrated manuscripts. See manuscriptsvaluation.com.

First editions and rare books — first editions of landmark Indian literary works in English and vernacular languages; first editions of significant Indian-authored international works.

Signed and inscribed books — books inscribed by the author, particularly where the inscription has personal significance or historical context.

Author correspondence — letters, postcards, and telegrams from significant Indian authors; valuation depends heavily on the significance of the correspondent and the content.

Literary archives — institutional-level collections of a single author's papers, notebooks, draft manuscripts, and correspondence; typically valued for donation to a university library or national archive.

Calligraphy — significant works in Arabic, Persian, Urdu, and Sanskrit calligraphy.

Comparables: Christie's and Sotheby's Books & Manuscripts sales; Osian's and William Dalrymple Collection sale records; specialist dealer catalogues.

6. Musical Instruments

Musical instrument valuation is a discipline that sits at the intersection of craft history, performing arts tradition, and fine-art market dynamics. In India, the category is particularly significant because the classical music tradition has produced instruments of exceptional craftsmanship and historical significance that are now in active collection and institutional acquisition. A2Z Valuers values:

Vintage sitars — by master luthiers including Riki Ram and Rikhi Ram; instruments with provenance connecting them to significant performers command substantial premiums.

Tanpuras, sarangis, and other plucked and bowed instruments — with valuation sensitive to age, condition, maker, and performance provenance.

Tablas and percussion instruments — vintage sets by significant makers.

Western instruments — vintage violins, cellos, pianos and keyboards with Indian-market provenance.

Ceremonial and folk instruments — shehnais, been, nagaswaram, and other ceremonial instruments of historical significance.

Comparables: Bonhams Musical Instruments sales; specialist dealer valuations; institutional acquisition records.

7. Photography as Fine Art

Fine-art photography is an established and actively traded arts category internationally, and is gaining significant traction in the Indian market. A2Z Valuers values:

Works by Indian fine-art photographers — Raghu Rai, Pablo Bartholomew, Dayanita Singh, Sooni Taraporevala, Ketaki Sheth, Gauri Gill and others whose work is in institutional and private collection.

Vintage photographic prints — 19th and early 20th century documentary photography from India, including colonial-era survey photography and early portrait photography.

Limited-edition prints — edition-numbered and artist-signed prints from significant photographers; edition number and print condition are primary value factors.

Photo-journalism archives — estate-level collections from significant photojournalists; typically valued for institutional donation.

Comparables: Saffronart's photography auctions; Christie's and Sotheby's photographs sales; specialist dealer records. See also photographsvaluers.com.

8. Film & Cinema Arts

India's cinema heritage is among the world's richest — and its material culture is an emerging but underserved arts valuation category. A2Z Valuers values:

Vintage Bollywood film posters — original lithograph posters from the 1940s–1980s golden era; print quality, rarity, and the significance of the film and its star cast are primary value factors.

Lobby cards and front-of-house stills — original production sets in period frames; rarity and completeness are premium factors.

Film props and production design objects — objects verified as used in specific productions; supported by production documentation.

Vintage film cameras and projection equipment — historically significant filmmaking equipment.

Film costumes — garments worn by significant performers in significant films; authentication and provenance are primary factors.

Comparables: AstaGuru's Bollywood memorabilia auctions; international entertainment memorabilia specialist auction records.

9. Digital Arts & New Media

Digital arts — including NFT-based artworks, video art, generative art, net art, and AI-generated works — is the newest and most volatile arts valuation category. A2Z Valuers provides valuation of:

NFT-based digital artworks — valued based on smart-contract records, platform sales history, artist reputation metrics, edition documentation, and prevailing market sentiment. Methodology disclosed as evolving; uncertainty explicitly stated.

Video art — limited-edition video works by significant Indian and international video artists; edition numbering, physical format, and display equipment are key factors.

Generative and AI-assisted art — an emerging category requiring bespoke methodology; A2Z Valuers applies a framework adapted from the print-edition market, with appropriate uncertainty disclosure.

Digital photography — high-resolution digital photographic works issued in limited editions.

Comparables: Platform sales records (SuperRare, Foundation, OpenSea) for NFT works; Christie's and Sotheby's digital art sales records; Saffronart's digital category. See also digitalpaintingvaluer.com.
