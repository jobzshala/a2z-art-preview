Why breadth matters in arts valuation

Most arts valuers in India specialize narrowly — a fine-art specialist who can value Husain and Souza but cannot competently value a Gond painting or a vintage sitar; a decorative-arts specialist who cannot value a literary manuscript. Nitesh Shrivastava has built a practice with genuine breadth — not breadth at the cost of depth, but breadth built on a rigorous methodology framework that adapts to each arts category's specific value drivers.

The same Government Approved Valuer who signs the certificate for a Husain canvas also signs the certificate for a Madhubani panel, a Raghu Rai photographic print, a first-edition Tagore, and a vintage Bollywood lobby card. That consistency of accountable authorship is the practice's defining quality.

Government appointments in arts valuation

Appointed by the Income Tax Department — for arts valuation during search-and-seizure proceedings PAN India.

Appointed by the GST Department — for arts valuation during investigation proceedings.

Appointed by the Narcotics Department — for valuation of arts assets discovered in enforcement proceedings.

Appointed by the Customs Department — for valuation of arts assets intercepted at Indian ports and border crossings.

Licensed under Section 34AB of the Income Tax Act, Rule 13 of the IT Rules, Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) of the IT Act, and the Black Money Act, 2015.

Notable arts valuation engagements

2025 — ₹50 Crore private arts collection: Valuation of rare Mughal jewellery and colonial Indian art for one of India's top auction houses, covered in the Indian financial press; followed by a record sale confirming the practice's position at the highest level of the Indian arts market.

Christie's benchmark tracking: Continuous monitoring of Christie's South Asian and Indian Art sales, including the landmark ₹119 crore M.F. Husain Untitled (Gram Yatra) sale on 19 March 2025 — the highest price ever paid for an Indian artwork.

Folk and tribal arts institutional valuations: Valuations for government bodies and institutional collectors of Madhubani, Warli, Gond, and Kalamkari collections.

Literary arts estate valuations: Manuscript and rare-book valuations for estate administration and charitable donation to Indian university libraries and national archives.

Performing arts memorabilia: Bollywood film poster and prop valuations for insurance and estate purposes.
