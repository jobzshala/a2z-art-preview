The breadth of “arts” as a valuation category

Every Art Form Is an Asset. Every Asset Needs a Valuation.

The word “arts” covers a spectrum that the Indian valuation market has historically addressed incompletely. Fine art and painting get the most attention — the auction records, the gallery primary-market data, the Christie's and Sotheby's comparables. But the Indian arts market is far wider than oil paintings and Mughal miniatures. It includes the entire tradition of Indian folk and tribal art — Madhubani, Warli, Gond, Phad, Kalamkari, Kalighat, Pichwai, Patachitra, Kerala Murals — a category that has seen significant institutional acquisition and collector interest but remains difficult to value in the absence of a specialist. It includes performing arts memorabilia — a growing global asset class that in India encompasses Bollywood film props, vintage cinema posters, concert memorabilia, and stage costumes from iconic productions. It includes literary arts — manuscripts, first editions, signed books, author correspondence, and literary archives with both monetary and heritage value. It includes musical instruments — where provenance, maker, age, and condition interact in valuation exactly as they do in fine art. It includes photography as a distinct and now actively traded art medium. And it includes digital arts and NFT-based works — an emerging and volatile category that requires a disciplined valuation methodology. A2Z Valuers values all of them. That is what artsvaluation.com is built for.

Arts Valuation Purposes

01

Capital Gain & Income Tax

Fair-market-value reports under the Income Tax Act for the sale of any arts asset — including 1 April 2001 base-date certificates under Section 55(2)(b) for legacy collections. Accepted by the Income Tax Department and ITAT. See capitalgainvaluation.com.

02

Insurance Valuation

Replacement-cost reports for arts insurance policies — covering fine art, folk art, decorative arts, memorabilia, instruments, and photographs. For the full insurance advisory, see artinsurancepolicy.com and insurancevaluation.co.in.

03

Estate & Family Division

Equitable-distribution valuations for inheritance, partition, and probate of arts collections — across all arts categories, including folk art inherited from regional traditions and performing arts memorabilia with both monetary and sentimental value.

04

Auction & Pre-Sale

Independent second opinions on auction-house estimates before consignment to Christie's, Sotheby's, AstaGuru, Saffronart, Pundole's, and DAG — for fine art, folk art, memorabilia, and decorative arts. See a2zauctionhouse.com.

05

Donation & Museum Gift

Donation valuations for gifts of arts assets to museums, cultural trusts, educational institutions, and archives — for NGMA, CSMVS, state museums, folk art foundations, and literary archives. See giftvaluation.com.

06

Litigation & Forensic

Reports for courts, tribunals, IT and GST search-and-seizure proceedings, customs assessments, matrimonial disputes, and arbitration — for all arts categories, including digital arts and performing arts memorabilia where specialist valuation expertise is hardest to find.

Arts Categories We Value

A2Z Valuers values arts assets across nine principal categories. Each is detailed on a dedicated category page; this section gives the overview.

Fine Art & Paintings — Indian modern masters, Bengal School, Mughal and Rajput miniatures, contemporary Indian art, European old masters.

Folk & Tribal Indian Arts — Madhubani, Warli, Gond, Phad, Kalamkari, Kalighat, Pichwai, Patachitra, Kerala Murals, Ganjifa, Thangka.

Decorative Arts — ceramics, glass, silver, bronze, textiles, carpets, furniture, ivory, jade, hardstone.

Musical Instruments — vintage sitars, violins, tanpuras, sarangis, tablas, and other historical instruments.

Film & Cinema Arts — vintage Bollywood lobby cards, film posters, props, costumes, and production memorabilia.

Digital Arts & New Media — NFT-based artworks, video art, generative art, and digital photography.
