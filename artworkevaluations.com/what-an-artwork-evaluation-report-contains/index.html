The Artwork Evaluation Report — What A2Z Valuers Delivers

A Government Approved artwork evaluation report from A2Z Valuers is a structured, legally defensible document. It is not a one-page letter of opinion. It is a complete investigative record that identifies the artwork, documents its condition, establishes its provenance, analyses comparable market transactions, applies a disclosed methodology, and arrives at a concluded value — all under the signature of Nitesh Shrivastava, Government Approved Valuer. The following are the ten elements every A2Z Valuers artwork evaluation report contains.

Element 1 — Artwork Identification

Artist name (or “attributed to” / “school of” where attribution is not certain), title, date, medium, support, dimensions (height × width, framed and unframed), and any inscriptions, signatures, stamps, or labels on the front, back, or frame. The identification establishes the artwork as a unique, specific object — distinct from all other works.

Element 2 — Physical Description

Detailed physical description of the artwork — the image, the palette, the surface quality, the support material, the frame (where present), and any distinguishing physical characteristics. Accompanied by colour photography under standard and raking light, documenting the work at the date of the evaluation.

Element 3 — Condition Report

Systematic assessment of the artwork’s physical condition — for paintings: canvas tension, paint-layer integrity, cracking, flaking, in-painting, cleaning, restoration, lining, and surface dirt; for works on paper: paper acidity, foxing, tears, creasing, fading, water damage; for sculptures and three-dimensional works: material integrity, joins, repairs, and surface condition. Condition is graded and the findings documented in writing and photography.

Element 4 — Provenance

Known ownership history of the artwork, from the artist or original creation to the current owner — with sources cited for each link in the chain. Where provenance is incomplete, gaps are identified. Antiquities Act status is noted for works that may be subject to the Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972.

Element 5 — Attribution Analysis

Assessment of the attribution claim for the artwork — whether the work is by the named artist, attributed to the named artist, from the school or circle of the named artist, or of uncertain attribution. Sources for the attribution assessment are cited: catalogue raisonné, scholarly literature, gallery records, auction-house attribution, or expert opinion where obtained.

Element 6 — Exhibition & Publication History

Record of the artwork’s known exhibition and publication history — exhibitions in which it appeared, catalogues in which it was published, and any awards or recognitions received. Exhibition and publication history is a primary provenance and attribution corroboration tool in Indian art market practice.

Element 7 — Basis of Value

Explicit statement of the basis of value applied in the evaluation — the definition under which the concluded value is expressed. The three principal bases used in Indian artwork evaluations are: fair market value (the price at which a willing buyer and a willing seller would transact, both with adequate knowledge and neither under compulsion); replacement cost (the cost of replacing the work with a comparable work in the current market); and liquidation value (the estimated realisable value in a forced or time-constrained sale).

The basis of value is linked to the evaluation purpose — fair market value for capital gain and estate; replacement cost for insurance; liquidation value for IBC.

Element 8 — Methodology & Comparables

Identification of the valuation methodology applied — typically the sales comparison approach (using comparable auction and dealer sales), the cost approach (for contemporary works where the artist’s current pricing is the primary reference), or a reconciliation of both where the data supports it. Comparable transactions are listed — artist, title, date, medium, dimensions, condition, sale venue, sale date, and hammer price or dealer price — with an analysis of how the subject artwork compares to each comparable on value-relevant characteristics.

Element 9 — Concluded Value

The evaluation’s concluded value — a specific figure (or a reasoned range, where market data does not support a precise point estimate), expressed in Indian Rupees at the valuation date, under the stated basis of value, for the stated purpose. The concluded value is the evaluator’s professional judgment, reconciling the methodology, the comparables, and the subject artwork’s specific characteristics.

Element 10 — Evaluator Certification

The evaluator’s certification — signed by Nitesh Shrivastava, Government Approved Valuer — stating that the evaluation was conducted independently, that the evaluator has no commercial interest in the outcome, that the methodology is disclosed, and that the evaluation meets the standards applicable to its stated purpose. The certification includes the evaluator’s Government Approved Valuer registration number, the valuation date, and the A2Z Valuers official stamp.
