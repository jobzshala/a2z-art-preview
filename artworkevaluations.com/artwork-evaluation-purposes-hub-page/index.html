Artwork Evaluation Purposes — Seven Purposes, One Evaluation Standard

Every artwork evaluation A2Z Valuers conducts is anchored to a specific legal or financial purpose — because the purpose determines the basis of value (replacement cost, fair market value, or liquidation value), the statutory framework (Income Tax Act, IBC, Antiquities Act, or general civil law), and the format and audience of the evaluation report. Click through for the full methodology, deliverables, and applicable standards for each purpose.

Purpose 1 — Capital Gain & Income Tax Artwork Evaluation

The most frequently requested artwork evaluation purpose in India. When an artwork is sold, the seller’s capital gain is the difference between the sale consideration and the indexed cost of acquisition. For artworks acquired before 1 April 2001 — which includes the majority of long-held Indian art collections — the cost of acquisition is the fair market value as on 1 April 2001 under Section 55(2)(b) of the Income Tax Act.

Establishing this base-date value requires an evaluation that draws on auction records, gallery price lists, dealer invoices, and period documentation from the relevant year. A2Z Valuers’ capital-gain artwork evaluations include the full computation: the base-date value, the CBDT Cost Inflation Index for the relevant years, and the concluded indexed cost of acquisition. See capitalgainvaluation.com and incometaxvaluation.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Sellers, HNIs, NRIs, Chartered Accountants, tax counsel, estate executors

Purpose 2 — Insurance Artwork Evaluation

An artwork insurance evaluation establishes the replacement cost of the insured work — the amount it would cost to replace the work with a comparable work of equivalent artistic quality, medium, date, and condition in the current market. This is distinct from fair market value (what the work would fetch at sale): for works by living artists whose prices are rising, replacement cost may significantly exceed recent sale prices.

A2Z Valuers issues artwork insurance evaluations accepted by HDFC ERGO, New India Assurance, Bajaj Allianz, ICICI Lombard, Tata AIG, United India Insurance, and international art insurers. For the full art insurance practice, see artinsurancepolicy.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Collectors, galleries, corporate art managers, institutions, insurers, brokers

Purpose 3 — Estate & Family Division Artwork Evaluation

An estate artwork evaluation establishes the fair market value of artworks in an estate at the date of death — the reference figure for inheritance tax purposes, for equitable distribution among beneficiaries, and for the executor’s duty of fair dealing. Estate artwork evaluations for family partition and family settlement establish the agreed value of each work so that the partition can be effected without court intervention.

Where the estate includes artworks subject to the Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972, the evaluation additionally identifies Antiquities Act registration status and flags works that may require ASI registration before transfer.

  • Typically engaged by: Estate executors, beneficiaries, family offices, probate courts, family court counsel

Purpose 4 — Auction & Pre-Sale Artwork Evaluation

An auction pre-sale artwork evaluation provides an independent second opinion on the auction house’s pre-sale estimate — useful for reserve-setting and consignment negotiations before a work is submitted to Christie’s, Sotheby’s, Bonhams, Phillips, AstaGuru, Saffronart, Pundole’s or DAG. The evaluation benchmarks the auction house’s estimate against independent comparables and flags any condition or provenance issues that may affect the final hammer price. For auction advisory, see a2zauctionhouse.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Consignors, estate executors, family offices, corporate art managers, dealers

Purpose 5 — Charitable Donation Artwork Evaluation

A charitable donation artwork evaluation establishes the fair market value of an artwork being gifted to a museum, cultural trust, educational institution, or charitable foundation — for the donor’s Income Tax Act position (Section 80G deduction where the institution is approved) and for the donee institution’s formal acquisition record. The evaluation must satisfy both the donor’s tax requirements and the institution’s governance standards — which means it must be independent, documented, and issued by a Government Approved Valuer whose credential is acceptable to both audiences. For the full donation valuation practice, see giftvaluation.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Donors, museum acquisition teams, cultural trusts, charities, tax counsel

Purpose 6 — Corporate & IBC Artwork Evaluation

A corporate artwork evaluation serves two distinct corporate purposes. For going-concern corporates, artwork evaluations support balance-sheet reporting under Ind AS 16 / Ind AS 38, insurance coverage, and asset-register maintenance. For distressed corporates in IBC CIRP proceedings, artwork evaluations under Regulation 27 of the CIRP Regulations deliver fair value and liquidation value to the Resolution Professional, the Committee of Creditors, and the NCLT — under the IBBI Valuation Standards and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017. See insolvencyandbankruptcyvaluers.com for the full IBC practice.

  • Typically engaged by: CFOs, audit committees, Resolution Professionals, financial creditors, NCLT-appointed liquidators

Purpose 7 — Litigation & Forensic Artwork Evaluation

A litigation and forensic artwork evaluation is prepared to the evidentiary standard of the forum that will receive it — the Delhi High Court, the ITAT, the NCLT, the DRT, or a matrimonial court — with methodology, comparables, and conclusions documented to withstand cross-examination by opposing experts. Nitesh Shrivastava is registered under Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) of the Income Tax Act and has been appointed by the Income Tax Department, GST Department, Narcotics Department, and Customs Department for forensic artwork evaluations during PAN India enforcement proceedings. His forensic artwork evaluation reports meet the evidentiary standards of the Delhi High Court, the ITAT, and the NCLT.

  • Typically engaged by: IT/GST/Customs respondents, matrimonial litigants, estate executors, Resolution Professionals, courts
