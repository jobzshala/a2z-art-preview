An Artwork Evaluation — What It Is, What It Does, and Why It Matters

The definition

An artwork evaluation is a formal, written assessment of an artwork’s value, conducted by a qualified and independent evaluator, that identifies the artwork, assesses its physical condition, researches its provenance and attribution, analyses comparable market transactions, selects an appropriate methodology, and arrives at a concluded value — expressed as a specific figure or a reasoned range — that can be relied upon by the party who commissions it and by the authority to whom it is submitted.

A2Z Valuers’ artwork evaluations are issued under the signature of Nitesh Shrivastava — Government Approved Valuer — which means they carry the legal weight of a Government Approved certificate under Indian law, accepted by the Income Tax Department, NCLT, the High Court, banks, insurers, and embassies.

An artwork evaluation vs other forms of art assessment

An artwork evaluation vs a dealer’s opinion: A dealer has a commercial interest in the transaction; an evaluator does not. A dealer’s opinion is not accepted by tax authorities, insurers, or courts. A Government Approved artwork evaluation is.

An artwork evaluation vs an auction-house estimate: An auction-house pre-sale estimate is a marketing tool, calibrated to attract consignors and buyers. It is not an independent evaluation and carries no statutory weight. An independent artwork evaluation is.

An artwork evaluation vs a certificate of authenticity: A certificate of authenticity addresses attribution — whether a work is by a particular artist. An artwork evaluation addresses value — what the work is worth at a specific date for a specific purpose. Both are sometimes needed; neither substitutes for the other.

An artwork evaluation vs an informal estimate: An informal estimate has no evidentiary weight. It cannot be submitted to a court, a tax authority, or an insurer. A Government Approved artwork evaluation can.

The three questions every artwork evaluation answers

What is it? — Physical description, attribution, medium, dimensions, date, condition, provenance. The identification that establishes the artwork as a unique, specific object.

What is it worth? — Concluded value at a specific valuation date, under a specific basis of value (replacement cost, fair market value, liquidation value) for a specific purpose. The figure that makes the evaluation legally usable.

How was that figure reached? — Methodology, comparables, assumptions, and limitations. The reasoning that makes the evaluation defensible when challenged.

Why “government approved” matters for artwork evaluations

In India, the designation “Government Approved Valuer” is a registered credential — not a self-description. It means the evaluator is registered under Section 34AB of the Income Tax Act, 1961, and is authorised to issue valuation certificates that the Income Tax Department is legally required to consider in assessments. It means the evaluator is registered under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 for IBC and Companies Act purposes.

It means the evaluation report carries a specific statutory presumption of independence and competence that an unregistered evaluator’s report cannot carry. Every artwork evaluation issued by A2Z Valuers under Nitesh Shrivastava’s signature carries this designation — which is why it is accepted by courts, tribunals, banks, insurers, and embassies that would not accept a comparable report from an unregistered source.
