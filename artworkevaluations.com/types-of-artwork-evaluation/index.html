Six Types of Artwork Evaluation — Matched to Every Client Situation

Artwork evaluations are not one-size-fits-all. The scale of the collection, the purpose of the evaluation, and the audience for the report all shape what the evaluation looks like and how it is conducted. A2Z Valuers offers six distinct evaluation types, each designed for a specific client situation. The standard of independence, documentation, and defensibility is the same across all six; what changes is the scope, the format, and the delivery.

Type 1 — Single Artwork Evaluation

The foundational evaluation — a focused, object-level evaluation of one artwork. Physical examination, condition assessment, provenance research, attribution analysis, comparables, and concluded value, delivered as a stand-alone evaluation certificate. The single artwork evaluation is the right choice when:

A collector needs a current evaluation of one specific work for insurance, tax, or estate purposes.

A buyer wants an independent check on a seller’s asking price before committing.

An estate executor needs a formal evaluation of a single significant work in the estate.

A donor wants a valuation of one work being given to a museum or charitable trust.

A litigant needs an independent evaluation of a specific work for court proceedings.

Turnaround: Standard 5–7 working days from inspection; expedited turnaround available for court deadlines.

Type 2 — Collection & Portfolio Evaluation

A systematic evaluation of an entire art collection or portfolio — from a private collection of 15–20 works to a family office portfolio of 50–100 works. Each work is evaluated individually using the single-artwork protocol; the results are assembled into a consolidated portfolio evaluation report with:

Individual evaluation certificates for each work in the collection.

A consolidated portfolio summary table — artist, title, date, medium, dimensions, condition grade, basis of value, and concluded value for every work.

Aggregate portfolio values by artist, by category, by date range, and in total.

A collection overview narrative — the evaluator’s assessment of the collection’s overall character, strengths, risks, and market positioning.

Collection evaluations are particularly useful for estate planning (where executors need a complete inventory with values), insurance scheduling (where the insurer needs a current sum-insured for each work), and family partition (where multiple beneficiaries need an agreed, independent valuation of all works). For art as a financial asset in a larger portfolio, see also artassetsappraiser.com.

Type 3 — Corporate Art Collection Evaluation

A structured evaluation of corporate-owned artwork displayed across offices, boardrooms, reception areas, client-facing spaces, and storage — for purposes that include:

Balance-sheet reporting under Ind AS 16 (Property, Plant and Equipment) or Ind AS 38 (Intangible Assets) — where corporate art is carried as a tangible or intangible asset and must be measured at cost or revalued amount.

Insurance coverage — ensuring the corporate art policy sum-insured reflects current market values.

Asset tagging and inventory management — linking physical artworks to asset register entries with current evaluation figures.

IBC and restructuring — fair-value and liquidation-value evaluations of corporate art collections in CIRP proceedings. See insolvencyandbankruptcyvaluers.com.

Corporate gifting and transfer — evaluations for art transferred between group companies or gifted to employees or clients.

A2Z Valuers conducts corporate art collection evaluations with a dedicated corporate-evaluation format that delivers the asset-register-ready data that finance and compliance teams need alongside the evaluation certificates that auditors accept. For LLP and institutional B2B engagements, see a2zvaluersllp.com.

Type 4 — Institutional & Museum Collection Evaluation

A formal evaluation of artworks held by museums, cultural trusts, universities, government cultural bodies, and public institutions — conducted to the standards required by:

Museum accreditation and re-accreditation — National Museum, NGMA, CSMVS, state museums, and regional cultural institutions.

Insurance for institutional collections — where the sum insured must reflect current market values for the institution’s governance to be satisfied.

Grant applications — where the evaluation of the collection underpins the institution’s stated asset base.

Donation acceptance — where an institution receiving a donated artwork needs an independent evaluation before the gift is formally accepted.

De-accession proceedings — where a museum proposes to remove a work from its permanent collection and needs an independent evaluation to support the governance process.

Heritage and conservation grants — where the evaluation of at-risk works supports applications for conservation funding.

Institutional evaluations are formatted to satisfy the governance and audit requirements of the relevant institution — not the same format as a private-collector evaluation certificate.

Type 5 — Pre-Purchase Artwork Evaluation

An independent evaluation of an artwork before the buyer makes a purchase commitment — the evaluation that tells the buyer whether the asking price is reasonable, the condition is as represented, and the provenance is clean. Pre-purchase artwork evaluations are commissioned by:

Private collectors buying directly from a gallery or at auction, who want an independent check on the gallery’s price or the auction-house estimate.

Corporate art managers evaluating works being considered for office acquisition, who need a figure for the capital expenditure approval process.

Family offices adding artworks to a managed portfolio, who need an independent evaluation before the acquisition is recorded in the portfolio.

Estate beneficiaries offered the option to purchase co-beneficiaries’ shares of an inherited work, who need an independent evaluation to set the price.

Institutions considering a purchase or acquisition at fair value, where governance requires an independent evaluation before the board approves the acquisition.

The pre-purchase artwork evaluation is distinct from a post-acquisition evaluation: it is conducted before ownership changes, specifically to inform the purchase decision. The evaluation addresses fair market value at the date of evaluation — the independent reference against which the asking price is assessed. It is not a guarantee of authenticity, though condition and provenance concerns identified during the evaluation are flagged. For dedicated pre-acquisition appraisal with USPAP methodology, see artworkappraiser.com.

Type 6 — Periodic Re-Evaluation Programme

A structured schedule of regular artwork re-evaluations that keeps a collector’s or institution’s evaluation documentation current as the art market moves. The Indian art market — particularly the Indian modern and contemporary categories — can move significantly over 12–24 months. A re-evaluation programme ensures that:

Insurance coverage remains adequate — avoiding the under-insurance gap that is the most common consequence of stale evaluations in the Indian art market.

Estate planning documentation reflects current values — so that a family settlement drawn on the basis of evaluations conducted three years ago is not immediately contested by beneficiaries who check current auction prices.

Corporate asset registers remain audit-ready — with current evaluation figures supporting the balance-sheet carrying value of art assets.

Capital gain planning is informed by current values — so that a collector considering a sale has a current independent evaluation figure when assessing the tax consequences of different timing scenarios.

Recommended re-evaluation frequency: Annual for high-value works (above ₹25 lakh per work) and actively-traded categories (Indian modern masters, contemporary art); biennial for stable categories (folk art, decorative arts, literary arts); event-triggered (at sale, inheritance, gift, or major market movement) for all categories.
