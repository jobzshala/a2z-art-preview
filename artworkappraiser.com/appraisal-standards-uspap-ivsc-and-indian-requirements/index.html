Appraisal Standards Applied to Artwork Appraisal in India

A credentialled artwork appraiser does not work from personal opinion. Every appraisal is conducted under a recognised professional standard that defines the methodology, the report format, the required disclosures, and the appraiser's ethical obligations. The standard applicable to any given engagement depends on the purpose of the appraisal and the audience that will rely on it. A2Z Valuers applies the correct standard for each engagement — and identifies that standard explicitly on the face of every appraisal report.

USPAP — Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice

USPAP is the internationally recognised standard for personal property appraisal, including artwork appraisal. It is published by The Appraisal Foundation (US) and updated biennially. USPAP compliance is required by:

US charitable donation appraisals — the IRS (US Internal Revenue Service) requires a USPAP-compliant appraisal for any charitable contribution of artwork valued at more than USD 5,000. Indian artwork donated by US taxpayers (including NRIs with US tax obligations) to US or Indian charitable institutions must be appraised to USPAP standard.

International insurance policies — AXA XL, Chubb, AIG, and Lloyd's syndicates writing fine art policies globally typically require USPAP-standard appraisals as supporting documentation.

International probate and estate proceedings — UK, US, Australian, and other common-law courts often require USPAP or equivalent appraisals for artwork in estates with cross-border elements.

International auction house pre-consignment due diligence — Christie's and Sotheby's, when receiving a consignment from an Indian client, will ask for a USPAP-standard appraisal as the seller's own independent assessment.

USPAP requires: a written report; identification of the client and intended users; definition of the intended use; definition of the type and definition of value; effective date of the appraisal; scope of work; description of the property; sales comparison analysis; and a signed certification. A2Z Valuers USPAP-compliant appraisal reports satisfy every one of these requirements.

IVSC — International Valuation Standards Council

IVSC — International Valuation Standards Council (IVS 2022)

The IVSC International Valuation Standards (IVS 2022) are the framework adopted by the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) as the basis for its own Valuation Standards, which apply to all IBC artwork appraisals. The IVS defines the bases of value (market value, investment value, synergistic value, liquidation value) and the valuation approaches (market approach, cost approach, income approach) that A2Z Valuers applies to artwork appraisals in IBC proceedings.

For non-IBC engagements, the IVS provides the international methodology framework that the practice aligns with across all appraisal contexts, adapted to Indian market conditions.

RICS Red Book — RICS Valuation: Global Standards

The RICS Red Book is the UK-based standard for valuation, used extensively in the UK, the GCC, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Australia. For NRI clients with UK-based assets or interests, for appraisals submitted to UK courts or probate authorities, or for UK-domiciled insurers writing policies on Indian art, A2Z Valuers prepares RICS Red Book-aligned artwork appraisals that satisfy the UK standard while remaining grounded in Indian market data.

Indian Requirements — Section 34AB, IT Act 1961

For all Indian Income Tax, IBC, Antiquities Act, SARFAESI, and Black Money Act purposes, the applicable Indian standard is the Government Approved Valuer registration under Section 34AB of the IT Act and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017. The Indian standard focuses on the basis of value (fair market value, replacement cost, or distress value as applicable), the documentation of comparables, and the valuer's certification of independence and qualification.

Nitesh Shrivastava is fully registered under all applicable Indian statutes — Section 34AB, Rule 13, Sections 132(2) and 132(9D), and the Black Money Act, 2015.

Which standard applies to your appraisal?

Sale of Indian art, Indian tax position — Indian (Section 34AB).

Charitable donation to Indian museum, Indian tax benefit — Indian (Section 34AB).

Charitable donation to US institution, US tax deduction (NRI) — USPAP.

IBC / NCLT Regulation 27 — IBBI Valuation Standards (IVS-based).

Insurance with international insurer (AXA XL, Chubb, Lloyd's) — USPAP or RICS Red Book.

Insurance with Indian insurer (HDFC ERGO, New India Assurance) — Indian (Section 34AB).

Pre-acquisition appraisal (no statutory requirement) — USPAP or IVSC (at client's preference).

UK estate or probate — RICS Red Book.

For any engagement where more than one standard applies — for example, a donation of Indian art by a US taxpayer to an Indian museum, which requires both USPAP and Indian tax documentation — A2Z Valuers prepares a single integrated appraisal report that satisfies both standards simultaneously.
