The appraiser distinction

An Artwork Appraiser Is Not the Same as an Artwork Valuer. Here Is Why the Difference Matters.

The Indian art market uses the terms “appraiser” and “valuer” interchangeably. They are not interchangeable. A valuer issues a statutory valuation report — a formally prescribed document under the Income Tax Act, the IBC, or the Antiquities Act, accepted by a specific Indian authority. A artwork appraiser does everything a valuer does, and more: the artwork appraiser physically examines the object, forms an expert opinion on attribution, condition, and authenticity, draws on current and historical market data from auction houses and the primary-market gallery system, applies a recognised appraisal methodology (USPAP, IVSC, or the RICS Red Book), and produces a comprehensive appraisal report that documents every step of the reasoning.

The appraiser's opinion is defensible not just because it is signed but because it is reasoned — and the reasoning is on the page.

When a collector wants to know if the asking price for a Husain canvas is fair before committing ₹1 crore, a statutory valuation certificate is not what they need. They need an artwork appraiser. When an estate administrator needs to divide a collection of twenty paintings equitably among three beneficiaries, a statutory valuation will serve — but an appraiser who has physically examined every work, noted every condition issue, and benchmarked every comparable will produce a result that holds up in front of a court. A2Z Valuers provides both — and artworkappraiser.com is where the appraisal expertise is documented.

Artwork Appraisal Services

01

Pre-Acquisition Appraisal

Independent appraisal before purchasing an artwork — is the asking price fair? Is the attribution correct? Is the condition as represented? Is there a provenance gap that creates legal or financial risk? The pre-acquisition appraisal is the only artwork appraisal that is entirely about protecting the buyer before the transaction.

02

Insurance Appraisal

Replacement-cost appraisal for new and renewal art insurance policies — and for claim defence after loss or damage. The appraisal certificate is the contractual foundation of the insurance policy. See artinsurancepolicy.com.

03

Capital Gain & Tax Appraisal

Fair-market-value appraisal under the Income Tax Act for sale of artworks — including 1 April 2001 base-date appraisals under Section 55(2)(b). See capitalgainvaluation.com.

04

Estate & Probate Appraisal

Object-level appraisal of every artwork in an estate for equitable distribution among beneficiaries — with condition notes, provenance summary, and individual market comparables for each work.

05

Charitable Donation Appraisal

USPAP-standard appraisal for donation of artworks to museums, charitable trusts, and educational institutions — the independent expert appraisal that the donee institution requires before accepting a significant gift.

06

Pre-Sale & Auction Appraisal

Independent second opinion on auction-house estimates before consignment to Christie's, Sotheby's, AstaGuru, Saffronart, Pundole's or DAG — for reserve-setting and consignment-term negotiation.

07

Litigation & Forensic Appraisal

Expert appraisal for courts, NCLT, ITAT, DRT, matrimonial disputes, and IT/GST search-and-seizure proceedings — the forensic appraisal that functions as expert evidence before the forum.
