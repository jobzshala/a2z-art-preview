Artwork Appraisal Services — A2Z Valuers

A2Z Valuers accepts artwork appraisal engagements across seven specialist service lines. Each is prepared under the appraisal standard the purpose requires — USPAP, IVSC, RICS Red Book, or Indian statutory — and signed personally by Nitesh Shrivastava, Government Approved Artwork Appraiser.

1. Pre-Acquisition Artwork Appraisal

The most important appraisal a collector can commission — and the one that collectors most often skip. The pre-acquisition artwork appraisal answers the questions that a buyer cannot answer from a gallery description or an auction catalogue entry alone: Is the attribution correct? Is the condition as represented? Is the asking price or the estimate at, above, or below fair market value? Is there a provenance gap that creates legal or financial risk? Is the work registered as an antiquity with the ASI, and if not, should it be? The appraisal is conducted before the purchase is committed. It is the only appraisal that can protect the buyer from a transaction they should not have entered.

The pre-acquisition appraisal delivers: attribution assessment; condition report; provenance review; fair market value opinion; comparison to asking price or auction estimate; Antiquities Act status (for works that may be antiquities); and a recommendation — acquire / proceed with caution / do not acquire — in plain language.

  • Typically engaged by: Private collectors, family offices, corporate art programme managers, gallery buyers, institutional buyers

2. Insurance Artwork Appraisal

A replacement-cost appraisal that establishes the sum insured for an art insurance policy — and forms the contractual foundation of the policy. Every major Indian art insurer — HDFC ERGO, New India Assurance, Bajaj Allianz, ICICI Lombard, Tata AIG, United India — requires a professional appraisal certificate as a condition of issuing a fine art insurance policy at the agreed value. The appraisal establishes the replacement cost (the cost of acquiring a comparable work at current market prices — typically higher than the fair market value of the insured work), the basis of settlement (agreed value or market value), and the baseline condition of the work at the date of the appraisal — the condition report that protects the policyholder in the event of a disputed claim. For the full insurance advisory, see artinsurancepolicy.com.

Recommended renewal cycle: every 2–3 years for actively-traded artwork categories, or whenever a benchmark sale for the artist moves the market significantly. The Christie's sale of M.F. Husain's Untitled (Gram Yatra) at ₹119 crore on 19 March 2025 reset the ceiling for the entire Indian modern master market — any Husain policy written before that date is likely underinsured.

  • Typically engaged by: Private collectors, galleries, corporate art managers, insurers, brokers

3. Capital Gain & Tax Artwork Appraisal

A fair-market-value appraisal under the Income Tax Act, 1961 for the sale of an artwork — the document the Assessing Officer requires for capital-gain computation. The appraisal establishes the fair market value as on 1 April 2001 under Section 55(2)(b) for legacy artworks (the most common request), or the fair market value at the date of acquisition for works acquired after 2001. The Cost Inflation Index is applied to arrive at the indexed cost of acquisition. The complete capital-gain computation is delivered as part of the appraisal report. For the dedicated capital-gain practice, see capitalgainvaluation.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Individual sellers, HNIs, NRIs, Chartered Accountants, tax counsel

4. Estate & Probate Artwork Appraisal

Object-level appraisal of every artwork in an estate — for equitable distribution among beneficiaries, for probate court proceedings, or for the executor's duty of fair dealing with the estate. The estate appraisal is the most thorough engagement type: every work in the estate is physically examined, individually described, condition-assessed, provenance-documented, and individually valued. The aggregate estate appraisal report provides the executor and the court with a complete, defensible inventory of the estate's art assets. For art asset-level portfolio assessment in larger estates, see artassetsappraiser.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Estate executors, beneficiaries, family offices, family court counsel, probate courts

5. Charitable Donation Artwork Appraisal

A USPAP-standard or Indian-statutory appraisal for the donation of artworks to museums, charitable trusts, educational institutions, and archives. The charitable donation appraisal is a uniquely demanding engagement: the donee institution requires an expert appraisal before it can formally accept a significant gift (to protect the institution from accepting a work that is worth less than its acquisition costs, or that carries legal encumbrances). The donor requires an appraisal for tax purposes (Section 80G deduction in India; charitable deduction under the US Internal Revenue Code if the donor has a US tax obligation). A2Z Valuers prepares donation appraisals that satisfy both the donee institution's requirements and the donor's tax authority simultaneously. For donation valuation, see giftvaluation.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Donors, donee institutions (NGMA, CSMVS, National Museum, state museums, university libraries), charitable trusts

6. Pre-Sale & Auction Artwork Appraisal

An independent second opinion on an auction house's pre-sale estimate — before committing to a consignment at a specific reserve. Auction houses have their own interests in setting estimates; those interests are not always identical to the consignor's. An independent pre-sale appraisal by A2Z Valuers establishes whether the auction house's estimate is at, above, or below current fair market value — and provides the consignor with the data to negotiate a higher reserve, a different auction, or no sale at all. Comparables benchmarked against the published catalogues of Christie's, Sotheby's, Bonhams, Phillips, Saffronart, AstaGuru, Pundole's and DAG. For auction advisory, see a2zauctionhouse.com.

  • Typically engaged by: Consignors, family offices, estate executors, galleries consigning on behalf of collectors

7. Litigation & Forensic Artwork Appraisal

Expert artwork appraisal for courts, NCLT, ITAT, DRT, matrimonial disputes, IT and GST search-and-seizure proceedings, ASI compulsory-acquisition proceedings, and arbitration. The forensic appraisal is the most demanding engagement type because the appraiser's opinion will be scrutinised by opposing counsel, challenged by a rival expert, and evaluated by a judge or arbitrator. Nitesh Shrivastava is registered under Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) of the IT Act as a forensic valuer — and has appeared in Income Tax assessment proceedings, matrimonial court valuation disputes, and government-agency enforcement engagements PAN India. His forensic appraisal reports are formatted to meet the evidentiary standards of the specific forum.

  • Typically engaged by: Counsel, courts, IT/GST/Customs respondents, matrimonial litigants, Resolution Professionals, arbitrators
