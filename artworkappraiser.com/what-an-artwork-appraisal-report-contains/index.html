What a Certified Artwork Appraisal Report from A2Z Valuers Contains

An artwork appraisal report is not a one-line certificate. It is a comprehensive professional document that records everything the appraiser examined, every source the appraiser consulted, every comparable the appraiser identified, and every assumption the appraiser made — before arriving at the concluded value. A report that does not show its working is not a defensible appraisal. Every A2Z Valuers artwork appraisal report contains the following ten elements:

1. Purpose and Intended Use

A statement of the purpose of the appraisal (insurance, capital gain, estate, donation, pre-acquisition, litigation) and the intended use (submission to a specific authority, institution, or court). This framing determines the applicable appraisal standard, the basis of value, and the disclosure requirements throughout the report.

2. Identification of the Artwork

A complete physical description of the artwork: artist (full name, nationality, dates); title (as inscribed or assigned); date of execution (as inscribed, documented, or appraiser-estimated); medium (support, ground, paint type, and any mixed-media elements); dimensions (height x width, with or without frame); signature and inscription (location, content, and assessment); and any stamps, labels, or identifying marks on the verso.

3. Condition Report

A professional condition assessment of the artwork at the date of appraisal, noting: overall condition grade (excellent / good / fair / poor); specific condition observations (losses, cracks, in-painting, relining, surface dirt, yellowed varnish, frame damage); evidence of restoration or conservation (re-touched areas, documented conservation treatments); and structural observations (canvas tension, panel warping, support integrity). Accompanied by condition photographs taken under standard and raking light.

4. Provenance Record

A documented ownership history of the artwork from the artist or original owner to the present: list of previous owners with dates and documentation sources; exhibition history (if available); publication history (if the work has been catalogued or reproduced in a scholarly publication); and Antiquities Act status (for works that may be antiquities under the 1972 Act, the registration status and any compliance implications).

5. Attribution Analysis

For works where the attribution is not beyond doubt, the appraiser's reasoned opinion on the attribution question: consistency of execution with the documented oeuvre; signature analysis; comparison with authenticated works; reference to catalogue raisonné or scholarly authority; and the appraiser's concluded attribution opinion and its confidence level.

6. Methodology Statement

A description of the valuation approaches applied — sales comparison approach (primary for most artwork appraisals), replacement cost approach (for insurance appraisals), and income approach (for artworks that generate licensing or exhibition income). The basis of value stated explicitly (fair market value, replacement cost, liquidation value, or other as the purpose requires). The appraisal standard under which the report is prepared (USPAP, IVSC, RICS Red Book, or Indian Section 34AB).

7. Comparable Sales

A selection of auction and gallery sales of comparable artworks, each cited with: auction house and sale date; lot description; medium and dimensions; condition; hammer price and buyer's premium; and the appraiser's analysis of how each comparable relates to the subject artwork (same artist, similar period, similar medium, similar subject, similar size, or higher/lower quality). Comparables drawn from Christie's, Sotheby's, Bonhams, Phillips, Saffronart, AstaGuru, Pundole's and DAG as relevant.

8. Concluded Value

The appraiser's concluded opinion of value — expressed as a single figure or a range — with the basis of value stated (fair market value as on a specific date; replacement cost; liquidation value). For capital-gain appraisals, the complete indexed cost of acquisition computation using the CBDT Cost Inflation Index.

9. Limiting Conditions and Assumptions

A statement of all assumptions made and limitations encountered in the appraisal: assumptions about title (the appraiser assumes good title without independent legal verification); limitations on physical access (if the work could not be physically inspected); reliance on third-party documentation (exhibition catalogues, provenance records provided by the client). Every assumption and limitation is disclosed on the face of the report — because a court or authority reviewing the report needs to understand the basis on which the concluded value was reached.

10. Appraiser Certification

A signed certification by Nitesh Shrivastava stating: the appraiser's qualifications and Government Approved Valuer registration details; a declaration of independence (no financial interest in the artwork being appraised; compensation not contingent on the value conclusion); a declaration of compliance with the applicable appraisal standard; and the effective date of the appraisal. Signed and stamped on A2Z Valuers letterhead with the principal valuer's certificate of registration.
