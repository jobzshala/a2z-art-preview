The Artwork Valuer’s Credentials — A Complete Reference

The authority of a Government Approved Artwork Valuer’s report derives directly from the credentials the valuer holds. This page documents every credential that makes a registered artwork valuer’s report legally actionable in India — what each credential is, what it requires, and what it means for the reports issued under it. All credentials described below are held by Nitesh Shrivastava, Government Approved Valuer, A2Z Valuers.

Credential 1 — Section 34AB, Income Tax Act, 1961

Section 34AB of the Income Tax Act, 1961 is the primary statutory provision governing the registration of qualified valuers for income-tax purposes. A registered valuer under Section 34AB must hold the prescribed qualifications for the relevant asset class (artworks and cultural assets), have a minimum period of practical valuation experience, pass the prescribed valuation examination, and maintain registration through renewal and continuing compliance.

The effect of Section 34AB registration is that the Income Tax Department must consider the registered valuer’s report in assessments, appeals, and search-and-seizure proceedings — and cannot substitute its own estimate without providing reasoned grounds for departing from the registered valuer’s concluded value. This statutory presumption is the most important practical consequence of the registration for the clients who commission artwork valuation reports.

Credential 2 — Rule 13, Income Tax Rules

Rule 13 of the Income Tax Rules prescribes the procedural and conduct standards for registered valuers: the qualification requirements by asset class, the application and renewal process, the grounds for deregistration, and the professional conduct obligations. A registered valuer must renew their Section 34AB registration periodically and maintain a clean conduct record. Nitesh Shrivastava’s Rule 13 registration is current, renewed as prescribed, and has never been subject to a conduct complaint or disciplinary proceeding.

Credential 3 — Sections 132(2) and 132(9D), Income Tax Act

Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) govern the forensic valuation of assets discovered in Income Tax search-and-seizure proceedings. Registration under these sections authorises the valuer to be appointed by the IT Department to value seized assets — including artworks and cultural property discovered during searches. This is the most demanding artwork valuation context in India: the valuer’s figure becomes the basis for determining the taxpayer’s undisclosed-asset liability, and the report must withstand the scrutiny of the taxpayer’s counsel, the ITAT, and the High Court.

Nitesh Shrivastava has been appointed under both sections for PAN India artwork valuations in search-and-seizure proceedings — a record of government trust in his forensic artwork valuation competence that no other artwork valuer in India can fully match.

Credential 4 — Black Money Act, 2015

The Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015 created a specific regime for the valuation of foreign assets held by Indian residents as undisclosed income — including art collections held overseas. The Act requires valuations by valuers licensed specifically for this purpose. Nitesh Shrivastava’s Black Money Act licence covers foreign-held artworks and cultural assets, enabling him to issue valuation reports for assessees who receive Black Money Act notices requiring the valuation of their overseas art holdings.

Credential 5 — IBBI Registered Valuers Panel

The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) maintains a Registered Valuers panel for valuers who issue Regulation 27 CIRP fair-value and liquidation-value reports in IBC proceedings. IBBI registration requires compliance with the IBBI Valuation Standards and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 — which prescribe distinct methodology requirements for IBC valuations that differ materially from Income Tax Act methodology.

Nitesh Shrivastava is IBBI-registered for artwork and cultural asset valuation, enabling A2Z Valuers to serve Resolution Professionals and Committees of Creditors across all NCLT benches in India.

Credential 6 — Bank Empanelments

The Reserve Bank of India’s empanelled-valuer framework requires banks to commission asset valuations — for credit decisions, NPA-resolution, and SARFAESI proceedings — from valuers formally empanelled by the bank under the RBI’s guidelines. Empanelment requires the bank’s credit and risk committees to have assessed the valuer’s credentials, track record, and methodology standards. A2Z Valuers is empanelled with leading nationalised and private-sector banks for art and cultural asset valuation — which means that credit officers and NPA-resolution teams at these banks can commission artwork valuations from Nitesh Shrivastava with the assurance that the report meets their governance requirements.

Credential 7 — MSME and ISO Certifications

A2Z Valuers holds MSME (Gold) certification and ISO 9001:2000 quality management certification — demonstrating that the practice’s internal processes, documentation standards, and quality controls meet externally audited international benchmarks.
