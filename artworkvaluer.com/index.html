The credential that makes the difference

The Report Is Only as Good as the Person Who Signs It.

Every artwork valuation report submitted to an Indian tax authority, court, bank, or insurer is examined for two things before anything else: who signed it, and what credential that person holds. A report signed by a Government Approved Valuer registered under Section 34AB of the Income Tax Act carries a statutory presumption of independence, competence, and accountability that no other document carries. The Assessing Officer is required to consider it. The NCLT bench expects it. The bank credit committee will not accept a substitute. A report signed by an unregistered dealer, gallery owner, or “market expert” carries none of these presumptions — and is frequently rejected outright. The difference is not procedural. It is the difference between a document that serves the client and a document that exposes the client to reassessment, penalty, and the cost of commissioning the report again from a registered valuer. artworkvaluer.com is the site that makes this distinction clear — and introduces the registered artwork valuer who can deliver the report that will be accepted.
