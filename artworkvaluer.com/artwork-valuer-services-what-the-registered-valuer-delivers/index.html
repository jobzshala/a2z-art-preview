Artwork Valuer Services — Six Services, One Registered Government Approved Valuer

Every artwork valuation service A2Z Valuers delivers is issued under the signature of Nitesh Shrivastava, Government Approved Artwork Valuer — registered under Section 34AB of the Income Tax Act, IBBI-registered, bank-empanelled, and government-appointed. The report is the registered valuer’s report: signed, stamped, accountable, and accepted by every Indian authority that relies on artwork valuation evidence.

1. Capital Gain Artwork Valuation

The most frequently requested artwork valuation service in India — fair-market-value reports under Sections 48 and 55(2)(b) of the Income Tax Act for the sale of paintings, sculptures, prints, folk art, and all artwork categories. For artworks acquired before 1 April 2001, the Section 55(2)(b) base-date valuation establishes the fair market value on that date; the indexed cost of acquisition is then computed using the CBDT Cost Inflation Index for the relevant years.

The report includes the full Section 48 computation. The Christie’s sale of M.F. Husain’s Untitled (Gram Yatra) at ₹119 crore on 19 March 2025 is among the apex benchmarks for Indian modern art capital-gain comparables. See capitalgainvaluation.com.

2. Insurance Artwork Valuation

Replacement-cost valuation certificates issued by a registered Government Approved Artwork Valuer and accepted by India’s leading art insurers — HDFC ERGO, New India Assurance, Bajaj Allianz, ICICI Lombard, Tata AIG, United India Insurance — and international insurers operating in India. The certificate establishes the sum insured per artwork, the basis of value (replacement cost), and the condition of the insured work at the certificate date. See artinsurancepolicy.com.

3. Estate & Family Division Artwork Valuation

Equitable-distribution artwork valuations for inheritance, partition, family settlement, and probate before Indian District Courts, High Courts, family courts, and probate courts. Formatted to the court’s evidentiary requirements and the executor’s duty of fair dealing. Where the estate includes antiquities subject to the Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972, the report identifies registration status and flags works requiring ASI disclosure. See artassetsappraiser.com.

4. IBC / NCLT Artwork Valuation

Regulation 27 fair-value and liquidation-value artwork valuation reports for Resolution Professionals, Committees of Creditors, and the NCLT — under the IBBI Valuation Standards and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017. Issued by an IBBI-registered valuer, in the NCLT-required format, with explicit liquidation-discount modelling for each artwork category in the debtor’s estate. See insolvencyandbankruptcyvaluers.com.

5. Forensic & Court Artwork Valuation

Forensic artwork valuation reports for Income Tax search-and-seizure proceedings under Sections 132(2) and 132(9D); GST and Customs assessments; the ITAT; matrimonial courts; the High Court; arbitration; and the DRT. Prepared by a valuer who is registered for forensic artwork valuation under Indian law and has been appointed by government enforcement agencies for PAN India proceedings. The report withstands the evidentiary scrutiny of the forum it is submitted to.

6. Pre-Sale & Auction Artwork Valuation

Independent second opinions on pre-sale estimates before consignment to Christie’s, Sotheby’s, Bonhams, Phillips, AstaGuru, Saffronart, Pundole’s, and DAG. The registered valuer’s independent figure establishes the consignor’s reference point for reserve-setting negotiations and identifies any condition or provenance factors that the auction house’s estimate may not have reflected. See a2zauctionhouse.com.
