The Court-Appointed Artwork Valuer — How Indian Courts and Agencies Appoint and Rely on Registered Valuers

Income Tax Department — Search and Seizure Appointment

Under Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) of the Income Tax Act, the Authorised Officer conducting a search may require a registered valuer to value any artwork or cultural asset discovered during the search. The registered valuer is appointed at the scene or immediately after, inspects the artworks in the presence of the panchas, issues a preliminary valuation on the spot (for the seizure record), and follows up with a full valuation report under the required format. The report becomes part of the official search record and determines the declared or undisclosed value of the seized artwork. Nitesh Shrivastava has been appointed under both sections for PAN India search proceedings.

NCLT — Resolution Professional Appointment

In IBC CIRP proceedings, the Resolution Professional is required by Regulation 27 to appoint two registered valuers independently to value the assets of the corporate debtor — one for fair value and one for liquidation value (or the same registered valuer issuing both, depending on the interpretation adopted). The RP selects registered valuers from the IBBI panel. The NCLT bench reviews the Regulation 27 reports as part of the information memorandum and the resolution plan approval process. Where artwork constitutes a material asset in the debtor’s estate, the NCLT expects the registered valuer’s report to address fair value and liquidation value separately, with explicit methodology and comparables.

Courts — Expert Witness Appointment

Indian courts — the Delhi High Court, state High Courts, District Courts, family courts, and the ITAT — appoint registered artwork valuers as expert witnesses under the Indian Evidence Act / Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam when artwork value is disputed in litigation. The court may appoint the valuer jointly at the parties’ agreement, or one party may appoint a registered valuer whose report the other party can contest with their own registered valuer’s report. In either case, the registered Government Approved Valuer’s report is the form of evidence the court is equipped to assess. Nitesh Shrivastava has provided expert artwork valuation evidence in matrimonial, probate, and income-tax proceedings before Indian courts.

Banks and DRT — SARFAESI and NPA Appointment

Under the SARFAESI Act, 2002, a bank seeking to realise artwork pledged as collateral must establish the reserve price through an independent empanelled-valuer report before auction. The bank’s SARFAESI authorised officer commissions the artwork valuation from the bank’s empanelled registered valuer, and the resulting report sets the auction reserve. Where the borrower contests the reserve price before the Debt Recovery Tribunal, the DRT assesses the empanelled valuer’s report as the bank’s evidence.
