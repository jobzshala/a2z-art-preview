The valuer behind the report

An artwork valuation report is only as authoritative as the person who signs it. Nitesh Shrivastava is that person — a registered Government Approved Valuer who has built India’s most credentialled, most widely appointed, and most institutionally accepted artwork valuation practice over more than two decades. He is not a market intermediary with an opinion. He is not a gallery owner with a price view. He is a Government Approved Valuer registered under Indian law, personally accountable for every figure on every report bearing his signature, and appointed by India’s own enforcement agencies to conduct the artwork valuations that matter most: the forensic valuations in Income Tax search-and-seizure proceedings where the assessed value of undisclosed artwork determines the taxpayer’s liability.

Professional background

Nitesh Shrivastava is a Civil Engineer by training — a discipline that instils the habit of measuring precisely, documenting rigorously, and standing behind a figure that can be verified. That engineering discipline is the foundation of his artwork valuation practice. His transition into artwork valuation was built on the recognition that the Indian art market — rapidly growing and highly heterogeneous — urgently needed valuers who combined technical rigour with market knowledge and statutory competence. Over more than two decades, he has built precisely that combination: a practice that values artworks from Indian modern masters to Mughal miniatures to Gond tribal paintings to Bollywood memorabilia to digital arts — under the same disciplined, documented, statute-compliant methodology applied to every engagement, at every value level.

Statutory registrations and credentials

Section 34AB, Income Tax Act, 1961

Nitesh Shrivastava is registered under Section 34AB of the Income Tax Act, 1961 as a qualified valuer for artworks and cultural assets — the primary Indian statutory registration for artwork valuers. Section 34AB registration means that the Income Tax Department is legally required to take his valuation reports into account in assessments and cannot dismiss them without reasoned engagement. It is the registration that converts an artwork valuation report from a private opinion into a statutory instrument.

Rule 13, Income Tax Rules

Registration under Section 34AB is governed by Rule 13 of the Income Tax Rules — which prescribes the qualification requirements, the renewal cycle, and the standards of conduct for registered valuers. Nitesh Shrivastava maintains full compliance with Rule 13 requirements, with registration renewed as prescribed and conduct records clear.

Sections 132(2) and 132(9D), Income Tax Act

Nitesh Shrivastava is registered under Sections 132(2) and 132(9D) of the Income Tax Act — the provisions that govern the valuation of assets seized or discovered during Income Tax search-and-seizure proceedings. Section 132(2) empowers the authorised officer to require a registered valuer to value any asset discovered during a search. Section 132(9D) governs the valuation of assets retained after a search. Nitesh Shrivastava has been appointed under both sections by the Income Tax Department for artwork valuations during PAN India search-and-seizure proceedings — the highest-stakes, most scrutinised context for artwork valuation in India.

Black Money Act, 2015

Nitesh Shrivastava is licensed under the Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015 to value foreign art assets held by Indian residents as undisclosed foreign income. This licence is held by very few registered valuers in India and reflects the Government’s assessment that his artwork valuation practice meets the standard required for the highest-sensitivity enforcement contexts.

IBBI Registered Valuers Panel

For IBC and Companies Act artwork valuation engagements, Nitesh Shrivastava is registered on the IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India) Registered Valuers panel — which is required for any valuer issuing Regulation 27 CIRP fair-value and liquidation-value reports before the NCLT. His IBC artwork valuation reports comply with the IBBI Valuation Standards and the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017.
