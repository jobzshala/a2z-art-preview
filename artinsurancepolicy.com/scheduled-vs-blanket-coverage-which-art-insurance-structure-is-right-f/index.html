Scheduled vs Blanket Art Insurance Coverage — A Valuer's Explanation

When structuring an art insurance policy in India, the policyholder and the insurer must choose between two fundamental coverage structures — or a combination of both. The choice has significant implications for the type of valuation required, the claim settlement process, and the risk of underinsurance. A2Z Valuers advises clients on the correct structure before issuing any valuation certificate — because the valuation methodology must match the coverage structure.

Scheduled Coverage

Scheduled coverage — also called itemised coverage — lists each artwork individually in the policy schedule, with a specifically agreed insured value for each piece. The insured value is typically set as either the agreed value (see the next page on Agreed Value vs Market Value) or the replacement cost at the time the policy is issued.

**Advantages of scheduled coverage:

Certainty at claim time — in the event of a total loss, the insurer pays the agreed scheduled value without dispute. No post-loss valuation argument about what the work was worth.

Higher per-item protection — individual works of significant value receive coverage commensurate with their actual worth, not diluted by a collection-wide average.

Appropriate for high-value individual works — a single Husain canvas worth ₹5 crore should be individually scheduled, not buried in a blanket policy.

Disadvantages of scheduled coverage:

Requires regular re-appraisal — as art market values change, the scheduled values must be updated (recommended every 2–3 years for actively-traded categories). An out-of-date scheduled value creates underinsurance risk.

Administrative burden — every new acquisition must be separately scheduled and appraised before it is fully covered under the policy.

The valuation requirement: every scheduled work requires an individual valuation certificate from a Government Approved Valuer before the policy is issued. A2Z Valuers provides these certificates within 24–72 hours for most individual works.

Blanket Coverage

Blanket coverage sets a single aggregate total value for the entire collection, without individually itemising each work. Claims are settled based on the current market value at the time of loss, rather than an agreed pre-loss scheduled value.

Advantages of blanket coverage:

Flexibility for growing collections — newly acquired works are automatically covered (up to the aggregate limit) without individual scheduling.

Lower administrative burden — the collection as a whole is covered without work-by-work itemisation.

Appropriate for gallery and dealer inventory — where the collection composition changes frequently.

Disadvantages of blanket coverage:

Post-loss valuation disputes — without a pre-agreed value, the insurer's surveyor and the policyholder's valuer may dispute the market value at the time of loss. This is the most common source of art insurance claim disputes in India.

Underinsurance risk — if the aggregate limit is below the current total market value of the collection (a common outcome as art values appreciate), the claim settlement will be proportionally reduced under average clauses.

The valuation requirement: a total collection appraisal establishing the aggregate portfolio value, updated at least annually. A2Z Valuers provides this as a standing annual engagement for gallery and corporate clients.

The recommended hybrid approach

For most serious collectors and institutions, A2Z Valuers recommends a hybrid structure: individually schedule all works above a defined threshold value (typically ₹10–25 lakh per work, depending on collection composition), and apply blanket coverage at an agreed aggregate for works below that threshold. This structure provides certainty for the highest-value pieces while maintaining flexibility for the broader collection. The valuation requirement is proportional: individual valuation certificates for scheduled works; total portfolio appraisal for the blanket layer.
