Agreed Value vs Market Value — The Most Important Decision in Art Insurance, and the Valuation It Requires

The Agreed Value basis

Under an agreed value art insurance policy, the insurer and the policyholder agree, at the time the policy is issued, on the insured value of each covered work. In the event of a total loss, the insurer pays the agreed value in full — without further dispute or negotiation. The agreed value is established at the outset and remains the basis of settlement throughout the policy period, subject to renewal re-appraisal.

Why agreed value is strongly preferred for fine art:

No post-loss valuation dispute — the single biggest source of art insurance claim friction in India is disagreement between the insurer's surveyor and the policyholder about what a lost work was worth. Agreed value eliminates this entirely.

Certainty of recovery — the policyholder knows exactly what they will receive if a total loss occurs, allowing them to plan replacements with confidence.

Recommended by India's leading art insurers including HDFC ERGO for their Art Insurance Policy product.

The valuation requirement for agreed value: A comprehensive replacement-cost or fair-market-value certificate from a Government Approved Valuer, issued at the time the policy is taken out, establishing the agreed insured value. A2Z Valuers provides this certificate — issued within 24–72 hours for most artworks.

The Market Value basis

Under a market value art insurance policy, the settlement in the event of loss is based on the current market value of the work at the time the loss occurs — not on a pre-agreed figure. The insurer appoints a surveyor who assesses the market value post-loss, and this assessment forms the basis of the claim settlement.

The risks of market value cover:

Post-loss valuation dispute — the insurer's surveyor and the policyholder's valuer may reach materially different conclusions about the market value at the time of loss. Without a pre-policy valuation certificate establishing a baseline, the policyholder is at a significant disadvantage.

Market timing risk — if the loss occurs at a point when the artist's market is depressed (which markets can be), the settlement may be below the work's peak value.

Practical advice from A2Z Valuers: even for market-value policies, always commission a professional pre-policy valuation certificate. This establishes the baseline from which the post-loss value should be assessed, and significantly strengthens the policyholder's position in any claim negotiation.

Art insurance premiums in India — what drives the cost

Art insurance premium rates in India are typically quoted as a percentage of the sum insured annually:

Premium range: 0.10% to 0.50% of sum insured annually, depending on the risk profile.

Factors that increase premium: high transit frequency, international transit, public exhibition, storage in non-specialised locations, absence of security systems, high claim history.

Factors that reduce premium: specialised storage (climate-controlled, fire-suppressed), professional security systems (CCTV, alarms, guards), art transport by specialist shippers, consolidated coverage with specialist insurers, positive claim history.

Premium example: a collection with an agreed insured value of ₹5 crore, kept in a well-secured private residence with climate control, could expect a premium of approximately ₹5–12.5 lakh annually.
