How an Art Insurance Claim Works in India — And Where the Valuer Makes the Difference

01

Immediate notification

In the event of any damage or loss, immediately notify the insurer — in writing or by calling the insurer's customer care number. Many Indian insurers require same-day notification. Delay in notification is one of the most common grounds for claim rejection or reduction. Call your insurer's 24-hour claims helpline as soon as the incident occurs.

02

Minimise further loss

Take all reasonable steps to minimise further damage to the artwork — moving it away from the source of damage, covering it, or placing it in controlled storage. Document these steps. Do not undertake any restoration or cleaning before the insurer's surveyor has inspected.

03

Surveyor appointment

The insurer will appoint a surveyor to inspect the loss. The surveyor assesses the nature and extent of the damage, determines whether the loss falls within the policy coverage, and makes a preliminary assessment of quantum. This is where the policyholder's pre-policy valuation certificate and condition report become critical. A surveyor who finds a well-documented, pre-agreed value is in a much weaker position to dispute the claim than one who is working against an undocumented sum insured.

04

Commission an independent valuer

This is where A2Z Valuers becomes your most important claim resource. Commission an independent valuation from a Government Approved Valuer — specifically, a claim-settlement valuation that establishes: (a) the fair market value of the work immediately before the loss; (b) the nature and extent of the damage; (c) the cost of professional restoration to pre-loss condition; and (d) any post-restoration residual value reduction. This report is the policyholder's primary evidence in the claim quantum negotiation. Call +91 9999992343 immediately upon notification of a loss.

05

Documentation submission

Submit all required documents to the insurer: the original valuation certificate, the condition report, the claim form, the police report (for theft), the transit documents (for transit claims), and the restoration expert's report (for partial damage). The completeness and quality of documentation directly determines the speed and quantum of settlement.

06

Settlement

The insurer processes the claim and offers a settlement based on: (a) the policy terms (agreed value or market value); (b) the surveyor's report; and (c) the policyholder's independent valuation evidence. For agreed value policies with a strong pre-policy valuation certificate, settlement is typically straightforward. For market value policies without a pre-policy valuation, settlement negotiations are more protracted. If the settlement offered is materially below the independently assessed value, A2Z Valuers can provide further expert evidence or appear as an expert witness in dispute resolution or arbitration proceedings.

The role of the Government Approved Valuer throughout the claim

A Government Approved Valuer from A2Z Valuers is not just useful at Step 4. The pre-policy valuation certificate (Step 0), the condition report (Step 0), and the claim-settlement valuation (Step 4) all sit within the practice's art insurance service. The best time to engage a Government Approved Valuer for art insurance purposes is before the loss — at the time the policy is taken out. The second-best time is immediately after a loss — before the surveyor's position hardens.
