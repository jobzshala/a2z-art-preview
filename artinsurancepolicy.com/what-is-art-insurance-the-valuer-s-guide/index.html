What Is Art Insurance — A Complete Guide from India's Government Approved Art Valuer

The definition

An art insurance policy — also called a fine art insurance policy — is a specialised insurance product designed to provide comprehensive protection to paintings, sculptures, antique furniture, musical instruments, rare books and manuscripts, collectibles, and other objects of art against risks including fire, flood, theft, accidental damage, transit damage, and other specified perils. Unlike a standard home insurance policy, which typically offers minimal coverage for valuable items (often capped at ₹10,000–₹50,000 per item), an art insurance policy provides coverage at the full agreed or market value of the insured work, and is designed around the specific nature, fragility and irreplaceability of fine art and collectibles.

Who needs an art insurance policy in India

Art insurance is appropriate for any individual or institution that owns, displays, stores, sells, or transports valuable art and collectibles:

Private collectors — individuals with paintings, sculptures, antiques, or collectibles whose value exceeds standard home policy limits.

Art galleries and art dealers — commercial spaces where art is displayed, bought, and sold; the inventory and the premises both require cover.

Museums and cultural institutions — permanent collections, visiting exhibitions, and works received on loan from other institutions.

Corporate art programme owners — companies with significant art on display in offices, boardrooms, and lobbies.

Artists — protecting works-in-progress, finished works in the studio, and works in transit to exhibitions or buyers.

Auction houses and sale rooms — consigned works require cover from the moment they leave the consignor until the moment they are delivered to the buyer.

Art shippers and logistics providers — transit cover for works in their custody during packing, transport, and installation.

What an art insurance policy covers

A standard all-risks art insurance policy in India covers any sudden and unforeseen physical loss or damage from any cause, other than those specifically excluded. The principal perils covered include:

Fire, lightning and explosion — including smoke damage from a fire.

Flood, water damage and storm — including roof leakage and burst pipes.

Theft and burglary — including attempted theft where damage occurs.

Accidental damage — including breakage of sculptures, tearing of canvas, scratching of surfaces.

Transit damage — damage during packing, loading, transport, unloading, and installation; including vibration and shock damage.

Exhibition damage — damage during public exhibitions from handling, lighting, or environmental exposure.

Earthquake and natural perils — loss or damage from seismic events, cyclones, and other natural catastrophes.

What an art insurance policy does NOT cover

What an art insurance policy does NOT cover (key exclusions)

Wear and tear, inherent vice and gradual deterioration — natural aging of materials is not an insurable risk.

Moth, vermin, rust, oxidation, warping or shrinkage — biological and environmental deterioration.

Sudden temperature change (unless caused by an insured peril like a storm or fire).

Loss in unattended vehicles — transit cover typically requires the vehicle to be attended at all times.

Inventory losses — shortfalls identified only on stock count, without a specific incident of loss.

Wilful acts and misconduct by the insured.

War, invasion and civil commotion (unless specifically endorsed).

Understanding these exclusions is critical. The most common reason art insurance claims are disputed or reduced in India is that the claimed loss falls into an exclusion category — and the absence of a proper valuation certificate that establishes baseline condition makes the dispute worse. A2Z Valuers' pre-policy condition reports provide the baseline documentation that protects policyholders at claim time.

The valuation certificate — the foundation of every art insurance policy

Every art insurance policy in India requires the insured and insurer to agree on the fair market value of each insured work, supported by a valuation certificate from a professional valuer. This is not a recommendation — it is a contractual requirement explicitly stated in the policy documents of leading Indian art insurers including HDFC ERGO, New India Assurance, Bajaj Allianz, ICICI Lombard, Tata AIG and United India Insurance. Without a current, professionally issued valuation certificate, the insurer's surveyor at claim time will make an independent determination of value — which may be significantly below the policyholder's expectation.
