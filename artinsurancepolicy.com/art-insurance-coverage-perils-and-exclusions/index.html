Art Insurance Policy · India

What Your Art Insurance Policy Covers — And the Exclusions That Catch Collectors Off Guard

Standard coverages under an all-risks art insurance policy in India

Accidental damage — breakage, tearing, chipping, scratching, impact damage. The most common claim in private collector policies; also the most frequently disputed where pre-loss condition is not documented.

Fire, lightning, explosion and smoke — including collateral damage from a fire in an adjacent property.

Flood, water damage, roof leakage and burst pipes — a significant risk for ground-floor and basement storage in Indian monsoon conditions.

Theft and burglary — including damage to the premises during a burglary where the artwork itself is damaged but not stolen.

Transit damage — damage during professional packing, loading, domestic transport, international transport, unloading, and installation. Standard policies cover on a wall-to-wall basis.

Exhibition risk — damage during public display, including damage from public handling, lighting proximity, or environmental exposure during the exhibition period.

Earthquake, storm, cyclone, and natural catastrophe — all-risks policies typically cover natural perils.

Restoration and conservation costs — where a work is partially damaged, the policy covers the cost of professional restoration, subject to the policy limit.

Critical exclusions — what Indian art insurance does NOT cover

Wear and tear, inherent vice and gradual deterioration — natural aging of canvas, paint, paper, or other materials; foxing in works on paper; crazing of old paint.

Moth, vermin, rust, oxidation, warping or shrinkage — biological and environmental processes.

Sudden temperature change — unless directly caused by an insured peril (e.g., fire suppression system activation, storm-broken window).

Loss from unattended vehicles — artworks left in an unattended vehicle are typically excluded from transit cover.

Inventory loss — a "mysterious disappearance" discovered only on stock count, without a specific identifiable incident of loss.

Wilful damage or misconduct by the insured or their agents.

Inadequate packing — damage during transit that results from sub-standard packing by non-professional handlers.

War, terrorism, nuclear risk — typically excluded unless specifically endorsed.

The condition report — your protection against exclusion disputes

The most common reason art insurance claims are disputed or reduced in India is the absence of a baseline condition report at the time the policy was issued. Without a condition report, the insurer's surveyor can argue that the damage predates the policy or constitutes excluded gradual deterioration — and the policyholder has no documentary defence. A2Z Valuers provides a condition report alongside every insurance valuation certificate — photographing each work under standard and raking light and documenting its condition at the date of assessment. This report is the policyholder's baseline defence against exclusion disputes at claim time.
